Chemistry — Study of Compounds C — Nitric Acid

ICSE Class 10 Answer Book

Generated 2026-09-10

Nitric acid is examined chiefly as an OXIDISING AGENT — that is what distinguishes it from the other acids, and it is why it does not give hydrogen with metals. The laboratory preparation and the reason for keeping the temperature below 200 °C were both set in the 2026 paper.

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (a) Cu — copper.

Nitric acid is a powerful OXIDISING AGENT, so as a rule it does not give hydrogen with metals: any hydrogen produced is immediately oxidised to water while the acid is reduced to an oxide of nitrogen. The two exceptions are MAGNESIUM and MANGANESE, which do liberate hydrogen with COLD, VERY DILUTE nitric acid.

Copper is not one of the exceptions — and it lies below hydrogen in the activity series in any case — so copper gives no hydrogen. It gives nitric oxide with dilute acid and nitrogen dioxide with concentrated acid.

Official Answer

Q2. (b) Carbon.

C + 2H₂SO₄ (conc.) → CO₂ + 2SO₂ + 2H₂O

Both products — carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide — turn lime water milky, which is why the question specifies TWO gases. Sulphur would give only sulphur dioxide, a single gas.

NOTE — this item is included in this chapter because it belongs with the oxidising behaviour of the concentrated acids; the analogous reaction of carbon with concentrated nitric acid also gives carbon dioxide.

Q6. (a) K₂SO₄. Below 200 °C the acid salt forms (KNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → KHSO₄ + HNO₃). Above 200 °C the potassium hydrogen sulphate reacts with more potassium nitrate to give the normal salt: KHSO₄ + KNO₃ → K₂SO₄ + HNO₃. The lower temperature is used deliberately, because the high temperature needed for the normal salt also decomposes the nitric acid and attacks the glass.

Q11. Nitric acid is a strong OXIDISING agent. Any hydrogen that would be set free by the metal is immediately oxidised to water by the acid, while the acid itself is reduced to an oxide of nitrogen (NO with dilute acid, NO₂ with concentrated). So the products are a nitrate, water and an oxide of nitrogen — not hydrogen.

(The exceptions are very dilute nitric acid with magnesium or manganese, which do give hydrogen.)

C. Short Answer Type

Q12. (a) Because nitric acid DECOMPOSES on strong heating, above about 200 °C, into nitrogen dioxide, oxygen and water:
4HNO₃ → 4NO₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O
The product would therefore be destroyed as fast as it was made, and the acid collected would be yellow-brown from dissolved nitrogen dioxide instead of colourless. A further reason is that above 200 °C the sodium hydrogen sulphate would react further and form a hard crust of sodium sulphate that attacks the glass apparatus and is difficult to remove.

(b) Because concentrated sulphuric acid is NON-VOLATILE, with a high boiling point, whereas nitric acid is VOLATILE. The less volatile acid displaces the more volatile one from its salt, and the nitric acid distils over and is condensed. Concentrated acid is used rather than dilute because water would dilute the product and because the reaction needs the acid undiluted to proceed.

Official Answer

Q13. Because on decomposition it readily gives up OXYGEN, oxidising other substances while itself being reduced to an oxide of nitrogen:
2HNO₃ → H₂O + 2NO₂ + [O]

The nascent oxygen so released is what does the oxidising, and it is the reason nitric acid does not behave like an ordinary acid towards metals: instead of hydrogen being liberated, it is oxidised to water.

EXAMPLE 1 — it oxidises a non-metal. Carbon is oxidised to carbon dioxide:
C + 4HNO₃ (conc.) → CO₂ + 4NO₂ + 2H₂O
(Sulphur is similarly oxidised to sulphuric acid.)

EXAMPLE 2 — it oxidises copper. With dilute acid the product is nitric oxide, with concentrated acid nitrogen dioxide:
3Cu + 8HNO₃ (dilute) → 3Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O
Cu + 4HNO₃ (conc.) → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O
In neither case is hydrogen evolved.

Q14. It is the test for a NITRATE.

To the solution suspected of containing a nitrate, freshly prepared FERROUS SULPHATE solution is added, and then CONCENTRATED SULPHURIC ACID is poured slowly down the side of the tilted test tube so that it forms a separate layer at the bottom.

A DARK BROWN RING appears at the junction of the two liquids, which confirms the presence of a nitrate. The ring is a complex of ferrous sulphate with the nitric oxide produced when the concentrated acid liberates nitric acid from the nitrate and the ferrous sulphate reduces it.

Q15. (i) In the manufacture of NITROGENOUS FERTILIZERS, chiefly ammonium nitrate and calcium nitrate — its largest single use.

(ii) In the manufacture of EXPLOSIVES — trinitrotoluene (TNT), nitroglycerine, gun cotton and picric acid — for which the nitration of organic compounds requires it.

(iii) In the PURIFICATION of gold and silver, and, mixed with concentrated hydrochloric acid as AQUA REGIA, in dissolving gold and platinum.

(Also acceptable: in the manufacture of dyes, drugs, perfumes and celluloid, and as an oxidising agent in the laboratory.)

Q17. The raw material is AMMONIA (from Haber's process).

STAGE 1 — catalytic oxidation of ammonia over a PLATINUM–rhodium gauze at about 800 °C:
4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O

STAGE 2 — the nitric oxide is cooled and combines with more air:
2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂

STAGE 3 — the nitrogen dioxide is absorbed in water in the presence of excess air:
4NO₂ + 2H₂O + O₂ → 4HNO₃

The dilute acid obtained is concentrated by distillation to about 68%.
