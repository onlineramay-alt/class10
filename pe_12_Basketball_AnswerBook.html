B. Terminology and Very Short Answer

Q3. (a) DRIBBLING — advancing the ball by bouncing it continuously on the floor with one hand. It is the only legal way for a player to move with the ball.

(b) PIVOTING — with the ball held and one foot (the pivot foot) kept in contact with the floor, turning the body by stepping with the other foot in any direction. It is legal and is used to protect the ball and change the passing angle.

(c) TRAVELLING (steps) — moving the pivot foot illegally, or taking more than the permitted steps while holding the ball without dribbling. The penalty is loss of possession.

(d) DOUBLE DRIBBLE — dribbling, stopping and catching the ball, and then dribbling again; or dribbling with both hands simultaneously. It is a violation and the ball is awarded to the opponents.

(e) THE THREE-SECOND RULE — an attacking player may not remain in the opponents' restricted area (the key or paint) for more than three consecutive seconds while his team is in control of the ball in the front court.

(Also worth knowing: jump ball, free throw, personal foul, technical foul, rebound, lay-up, the 24-second shot clock, and the 8-second rule for advancing to the front court.)

Q6. (i) DRIBBLING. Pushing the ball down with the FINGER PADS, not slapping it with the palm, the wrist doing the work and the ball kept below waist height for control. The head is up to see the court, and the non-dribbling arm protects the ball. Variations: the speed dribble, the crossover, the behind-the-back and the reverse dribble to change direction.

(ii) PASSING. The CHEST PASS — ball held at chest height, elbows in, pushed out with both hands and a snap of the wrists so the thumbs finish pointing down. The BOUNCE PASS, aimed to strike the floor about two-thirds of the way to the receiver, useful against a tall defender. The OVERHEAD PASS for distance, and the baseball pass for a fast break.

(iii) SHOOTING. The set shot and jump shot — feet shoulder-width, knees bent, the shooting hand under the ball and the other steadying it at the side, the elbow beneath the ball, and a full extension with the wrist flicked so the ball is released with backspin. The LAY-UP — approaching at an angle, taking off from the foot opposite the shooting hand, and laying the ball off the backboard.

(iv) PIVOTING AND FOOTWORK. Keeping one foot planted as the pivot and turning on it to protect the ball, create a passing angle or set up a shot; together with the stop (jump stop and stride stop), the change of pace and the defensive slide, in which the feet never cross.

(Also acceptable: REBOUNDING — boxing out the opponent, timing the jump and catching with two hands; and DEFENCE — the low stance, hands active, staying between opponent and basket.)
