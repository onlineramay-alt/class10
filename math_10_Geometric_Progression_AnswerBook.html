nTH TERM: aₙ = ar^(n−1)
SUM OF n TERMS: Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) when r > 1, and a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) when r < 1.
SUM TO INFINITY: S∞ = a/(1 − r), valid only when |r| < 1.

In an AP you ADD d each time; in a GP you MULTIPLY by r. Check which you have by testing whether successive differences or successive ratios are constant.

Q1. a = 3
r = 6 ÷ 3 = 2 (check: 12 ÷ 6 = 2 ✓ and 24 ÷ 12 = 2 ✓, so it is a GP)
n = 8

8TH TERM:
aₙ = ar^(n−1)
a₈ = 3 × 2⁷ = 3 × 128 = **384**

SUM OF 8 TERMS — use the r > 1 form:
Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1)
S₈ = 3(2⁸ − 1)/(2 − 1)
= 3(256 − 1)/1
= 3 × 255
= **765**

CHECK by adding: 3 + 6 + 12 + 24 + 48 + 96 + 192 + 384 = 765 ✓

Q2. a₃ = ar² = 12 …(i)
a₆ = ar⁵ = 96 …(ii)

DIVIDE (ii) by (i) — this eliminates a, which is the standard move in every GP problem of this type:
ar⁵ / ar² = 96 / 12
r³ = 8
**r = 2**

Substitute into (i):
a(2)² = 12
4a = 12
**a = 3**

The GP is 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, …

CHECK: the 3rd term is 12 ✓ and the 6th is 96 ✓

NOTE the contrast with an AP: there you SUBTRACT the two equations to eliminate a, here you DIVIDE them.

Q3. a = 6
r = 2 ÷ 6 = 1/3 (check: (2/3) ÷ 2 = 1/3 ✓)

FIRST CHECK THAT THE SUM EXISTS. The sum to infinity is defined only when |r| < 1. Here |1/3| < 1, so it exists.

S∞ = a / (1 − r)
= 6 / (1 − 1/3)
= 6 / (2/3)
= 6 × 3/2
= **9**

STATING THE CONDITION IS PART OF THE ANSWER. If |r| ≥ 1 the terms do not shrink, the sum grows without limit, and there is no sum to infinity — a question may be set precisely to test whether you check.
