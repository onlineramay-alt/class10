Question 2 of every paper gives ten one-mark map-marking items on an outline map of India — ten marks, compulsory, and the most predictable marks in the whole examination. The syllabus lists exactly what may be asked, and the papers draw from that list every year. The complete syllabus list is reproduced below as a checklist; practise it on a blank outline until every item can be placed without hesitation.

B. Outline Map Marking (from the papers)

Q3. (i) NILGIRIS — the block of hills where the Western and Eastern Ghats meet, in the far south, at the junction of Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka.
(ii) GULF OF KHAMBHAT — the inlet of the Arabian Sea on the coast of Gujarat, east of the Kathiawar peninsula. (The Gulf of Kutch is the one to the west of it — do not confuse them.)
(iii) DENSELY POPULATED REGION IN THE EAST — shade the lower Ganga valley and delta, that is West Bengal and Bihar. The Gangetic plain is the standard answer.
(iv) SOIL KNOWN FOR COTTON — BLACK soil, also called regur, in the Deccan lava region: Maharashtra, western Madhya Pradesh and parts of Gujarat.
(v) KANARA COASTAL PLAIN — the western coastal strip of Karnataka, between the Konkan coast to the north and the Malabar coast to the south.
(vi) WINDS BRINGING WINTER RAIN TO CHENNAI — the NORTH-EAST MONSOON, or retreating monsoon. Draw arrows over the Bay of Bengal pointing from the north-east towards the Coromandel coast, and label them.
(vii) SINGHBHUM — in Jharkhand; mark with S. It is the iron-ore region named in the syllabus.
(viii) MAHANADI — rises in Chhattisgarh and flows east into the Bay of Bengal at Odisha.
(ix) INLAND FRESHWATER LAKE — Wular Lake in Kashmir. (Chilka is a lagoon and brackish, so it does not answer this question — the examiner is testing that distinction.)
(x) KOCHI — on the Malabar coast of Kerala.

GENERAL RULES FOR THIS QUESTION. Read the instruction word: 'mark and label' means a point or line with its name; 'shade and label' means an area coloured or hatched and named; 'mark with arrows' means direction must be shown. Use arrows to lead labels away from crowded areas, as the paper's own note instructs. Write names in small neat capitals along the feature, not across other features. And detach the map and fasten it to the answer booklet — candidates lose the whole ten marks by forgetting.

C. Short Answer Type

Q7. GULF OF KUTCH lies to the NORTH-WEST of the Kathiawar (Saurashtra) peninsula in Gujarat, separating it from the Kutch region. GULF OF KHAMBHAT lies to the SOUTH-EAST of the same peninsula, at the head of which the Sabarmati, Mahi, Narmada and Tapti drain. Both are inlets of the Arabian Sea, on either side of the same peninsula — which is exactly why candidates confuse them.

CHILKA LAKE is on the coast of Odisha, south of the Mahanadi delta. It is a LAGOON, separated from the Bay of Bengal by a sand bar, and its water is BRACKISH. It is the largest lagoon in India.

WULAR LAKE is in Kashmir, fed by the Jhelum. It is an INLAND FRESHWATER lake, and the largest freshwater lake in India.

The distinction matters: when a paper asks for 'an inland freshwater lake' (ICSE 2025 Improvement, Q2(ix)) the answer is Wular and not Chilka.
