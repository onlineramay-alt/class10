B. Terminology and Very Short Answer

Q3. (a) ACE — a service that lands in the opponents' court untouched, or that they cannot play, so that the serving side wins the point directly from the serve.

(b) DIG — a defensive underhand pass, played with the forearms joined, used to receive a hard-driven ball, particularly a spike, and keep it in play.

(c) SPIKE (smash) — the principal attacking stroke: a player jumps and strikes the ball downwards forcefully into the opponents' court with an open hand, usually as the third contact.

(d) BLOCK — one, two or three players at the net jumping with arms raised above it to intercept a spike and deflect it back into the opponents' court. A touch on the block does not count as one of the team's three contacts.

(e) LIBERO — a specialist defensive player who wears a contrasting shirt, may replace any back-row player without counting as a substitution, and may not serve, block or attack the ball above the height of the net.

(Also worth knowing: set or volley, rotation, foot fault, four hits, carrying or lifting, and the rally-point system.)

Q4. GOVERNING BODIES.
National: the VOLLEYBALL FEDERATION OF INDIA (VFI).
International: FIVB — the Fédération Internationale de Volleyball.

TOURNAMENTS.
National: the SENIOR NATIONAL VOLLEYBALL CHAMPIONSHIP (also acceptable — the Federation Cup, the Prime Volleyball League).
International: the OLYMPIC GAMES volleyball tournament (also acceptable — the FIVB World Championship, the FIVB Volleyball Nations League, the Asian Games).

SCORING. The RALLY-POINT system: a point is scored on every rally, whichever side served. A match is the best of FIVE sets. The first four sets are played to 25 POINTS, and the deciding fifth set to 15 POINTS. A set must be won by a margin of at least TWO points, so play continues beyond 25 (or 15) until a two-point lead is established.

Q5. WHAT THE DIAGRAM MUST SHOW, with these measurements labelled:

THE COURT. A rectangle 18 m long by 9 m wide, divided by the net into two square halves of 9 m × 9 m.

MARKINGS.
— The CENTRE LINE beneath the net, dividing the court in two.
— The ATTACK LINE (3-metre line) in each half, 3 m from and parallel to the centre line. It separates the front zone from the back zone, and a back-row player may not attack the ball above net height from in front of it.
— The SERVICE ZONE, 9 m wide, behind each end line.
— The FREE ZONE surrounding the court, at least 3 m on all sides (5 m at the sides and 6.5 m behind for international competition), with a clear playing space at least 7 m high.

THE NET.
— HEIGHT at the centre: 2.43 m for MEN, 2.24 m for WOMEN.

Q6. (i) SERVICE. The stroke that puts the ball in play, struck from behind the end line. The UNDERARM service is the simplest and most reliable; the OVERARM (tennis) service is struck above the head with more power; the FLOAT service is hit with no spin so that the ball wobbles unpredictably; and the JUMP service adds power by leaping into the strike.

(ii) PASS or DIG (underarm pass). The forearms held together to make a flat platform, the knees bent, the ball played off the forearms rather than the hands. Used to receive the service and to defend against the spike — the first contact of almost every rally.

(iii) SET or VOLLEY (overhead pass). The ball played above the forehead with the fingers of both hands forming a triangle, the wrists giving and then extending. It is the second contact, whose purpose is to place the ball accurately for the spiker.

(iv) SPIKE or SMASH. The attacking stroke and the usual third contact: an approach run, a two-footed take-off, a high jump, and the ball struck downwards with an open hand and a snap of the wrist so that it drops steeply into the opponents' court.

(v) BLOCK. Defence at the net: one or more players jump with arms straight and hands spread above the net to stop the spike, penetrating over the net where the rules allow. It is the first line of defence and is not counted as one of the three contacts.

THE RHYTHM OF THE RALLY, which a good answer mentions: dig — set — spike. Three touches, no more, and no player may touch the ball twice in succession.
