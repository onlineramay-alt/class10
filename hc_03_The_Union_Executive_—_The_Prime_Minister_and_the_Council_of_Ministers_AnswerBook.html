C. Short Answer Type

Q20. (i) Size and composition — the Council of Ministers is the whole body of ministers, comprising Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers; the Cabinet is only the inner ring of the most senior ministers, who hold the important portfolios. (ii) Meetings — the Council of Ministers as a whole almost never meets; the Cabinet meets regularly, often weekly, and its decisions are the decisions of the government. (iii) Powers — real policy is made in the Cabinet, and the Council of Ministers as a body merely carries it out. The Cabinet is mentioned in the Constitution only since the 44th Amendment, whereas the Council of Ministers is the body named in Article 74.

Q24. It means that the ministers are answerable to the Lok Sabha not individually but as a single body. Policy is settled collectively in the Cabinet, and once a decision is taken every minister is bound to defend it in public, whatever he may have argued in private; a minister who cannot do so must resign. If the Lok Sabha passes a vote of no-confidence against any one minister, or rejects a government Bill of importance, the entire Council of Ministers must resign. This is the principle that makes the executive continuously accountable to the Legislature.

Official Answer

Q25. Collective responsibility is owed to the Lok Sabha by the Council of Ministers as a body: they stand or fall together, and a vote of no-confidence against one of them brings down the whole government. Individual responsibility is owed by each minister to the President for the working of his own department: he answers in Parliament for its administration, and the President may, on the Prime Minister's advice, dismiss him alone without the government falling. In short, collective responsibility is political and to the House; individual responsibility is departmental and to the President.
