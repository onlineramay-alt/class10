Q52. The sex of the child does depend on the father — since the mother's egg always carries an X-chromosome, it is the father's sperm (X-bearing or Y-bearing) that decides whether the zygote is XX (female) or XY (male). At the same time, which type of sperm (X-bearing or Y-bearing) succeeds in fertilising the egg is a matter of chance, as roughly equal numbers of both types are produced and either has an equal probability of fertilising the ovum. So it is correct to say the sex of the child is determined by the father's sperm, but which sperm fertilises the egg is left to chance.

Official Answer

Q53. (a) Karyotype is the characteristic number, size and shape of the chromosome complement of a species/cell (a representation of chromosomes arranged in pairs); Karyokinesis is the division of the nucleus during cell division. (b) Autosomes are the chromosomes (22 pairs in humans) that control general body/somatic characters and are identical in both sexes; Sex chromosomes (1 pair, XX or XY) determine the sex of the individual. (c) A pair of Homozygous chromosomes carries identical/similar alleles for a character (e.g. RR or rr); a pair of Heterozygous chromosomes carries dissimilar/different alleles for the same character (e.g. Rr).
