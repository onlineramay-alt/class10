B. Identify / Name / Very Short Answer

Q12. (a) A MINERAL — a naturally occurring substance, obtained by mining, in which a metal occurs in the combined or free state, along with earthy impurities.

(b) An ORE — a mineral from which a metal can be extracted PROFITABLY and CONVENIENTLY. Every ore is a mineral, but not every mineral is an ore.

(c) GANGUE or MATRIX — the earthy and unwanted impurities associated with the ore, such as sand, clay and rock, which must be removed.

(d) FLUX — a substance added to the furnace which combines with the infusible impurities to form a fusible SLAG that can be run off. Limestone (CaCO₃) is the flux used with an acidic impurity such as silica.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q13. (a) BAUXITE is concentrated by the BAYER'S PROCESS, a chemical method. It depends on the AMPHOTERIC nature of aluminium oxide: the powdered ore is treated with hot concentrated sodium hydroxide, in which the alumina dissolves as sodium aluminate while the impurities — chiefly ferric oxide and silica — remain undissolved and are filtered off. The aluminate is then diluted and seeded to precipitate pure aluminium hydroxide, which is heated to give pure alumina.

(b) A SULPHIDE ORE is concentrated by FROTH FLOTATION. It depends on the fact that the sulphide ore particles are preferentially WETTED BY OIL while the gangue is wetted by water. The powdered ore is agitated with water, pine oil and a stream of air; the ore particles cling to the froth and rise to the surface, where they are skimmed off, and the gangue sinks.

C. Short Answer Type

Q19. (i) CONCENTRATION or DRESSING OF THE ORE — the removal of the gangue, by physical methods such as hydraulic washing, magnetic separation and froth flotation, or by chemical methods such as Bayer's process.

(ii) CONVERSION OF THE CONCENTRATED ORE TO THE OXIDE — because the oxide is the form most easily reduced. This is done by CALCINATION, heating in the absence or limited supply of air, used for carbonate ores and to drive off water; or by ROASTING, heating strongly in a free supply of air, used for sulphide ores.

(iii) REDUCTION OF THE OXIDE TO THE METAL, followed by REFINING. The method depends on the metal's position in the activity series: highly reactive metals such as sodium, aluminium and magnesium are obtained by ELECTROLYTIC reduction; moderately reactive metals such as iron, zinc and lead by reduction with CARBON or carbon monoxide; and the least reactive by simple heating. Refining is usually electrolytic.

Q20. Because aluminium is a HIGHLY REACTIVE metal, standing near the top of the activity series and well ABOVE carbon. It holds its oxygen so strongly that carbon cannot take it away — carbon is the weaker reducing agent of the two, so the reaction will not go. Attempting it at high temperature produces aluminium carbide instead.

Aluminium is therefore extracted by ELECTROLYTIC REDUCTION — the HALL–HÉROULT process — in which pure alumina is dissolved in molten cryolite and electrolysed. Only electricity is a strong enough reducing agent for a metal of this reactivity, which is why aluminium was a precious metal until cheap electric power made the process possible.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q21. THE ELECTROLYTE. Pure alumina (Al₂O₃) obtained from bauxite, dissolved in molten CRYOLITE (Na₃AlF₆) with about 10 per cent FLUORSPAR (CaF₂). Alumina alone melts at about 2050 °C, which is impracticable; dissolving it in cryolite brings the working temperature down to about 950 °C and makes the melt a much better conductor. That is the whole reason for the cryolite.

THE ELECTRODES. Both are of GRAPHITE. The CATHODE is the graphite lining of the iron tank itself; the ANODES are thick graphite rods dipping into the melt from above.

AT THE CATHODE (reduction):
Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
Molten aluminium collects at the bottom of the cell and is tapped off.

AT THE ANODE (oxidation):
2O²⁻ − 4e⁻ → O₂
The oxygen liberated attacks the hot graphite anodes, burning them away to carbon dioxide, so the anodes must be REPLACED PERIODICALLY. This is a standard examination point.

The aluminium obtained is about 99.5 per cent pure and is further purified by Hoope's electrolytic process.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q22. An ALLOY is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or of a metal with a non-metal, fused together in a definite proportion.

WHY THEY ARE MADE — to obtain properties the pure metal lacks: greater HARDNESS and STRENGTH (pure gold is too soft for jewellery); greater RESISTANCE TO CORROSION (stainless steel does not rust); a LOWER MELTING POINT for solders; a better APPEARANCE and lustre; and a lower DENSITY combined with strength, which is what aircraft construction needs.

BRASS — copper about 70 per cent with zinc about 30 per cent. Used for utensils, decorative articles, machine parts and electrical fittings, being harder than copper and resistant to corrosion.

DURALUMIN — aluminium about 95 per cent with copper 4 per cent, magnesium and manganese about 0.5 per cent each. Used in AIRCRAFT and spacecraft parts, and in pressure cookers, because it is nearly as light as aluminium but far stronger.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q23. CALCINATION is the strong heating of an ore in a LIMITED SUPPLY or ABSENCE of air, to drive off water, carbon dioxide and other volatile matter and convert a carbonate or hydroxide ore to the oxide. Example: heating bauxite, or the carbonate ore calamine (ZnCO₃).

ROASTING is the strong heating of an ore in a FREE (excess) SUPPLY of air, so that the ore is oxidised. It is used for SULPHIDE ores, converting them to the oxide with the escape of sulphur dioxide. Example: roasting zinc blende (ZnS) to zinc oxide.

The key difference is the supply of air — limited for calcination, plentiful for roasting.

Q24. (a) A reducing agent gives up electrons. Down a group the atoms get larger and the outer electrons are held less tightly, so they are lost more readily — the elements become better electron donors, i.e. stronger reducing agents.
