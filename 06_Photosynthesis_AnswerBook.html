Q60. The Calvin cycle (also called the Calvin-Benson cycle, or the photosynthetic carbon-reduction cycle) is the set of light-independent biochemical reactions, occurring in the stroma of the chloroplast, in which carbon dioxide is progressively reduced (using the ATP and NADPH produced in the light reaction) and fixed into carbohydrate, with the enzyme RuBisCO catalysing the initial fixation step.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q61. Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors (1905) states that when a physiological process (such as photosynthesis) depends on several separate factors, the rate of that process is limited by the factor which is nearest to (or at) its minimum value at that time — i.e. the 'limiting factor' — regardless of how favourable the other factors are; increasing the limiting factor increases the rate, until another factor in turn becomes limiting.
