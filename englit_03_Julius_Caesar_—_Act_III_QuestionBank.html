B. Extract-Based / Context Questions

Q10. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

CAESAR:
I could be well moved, if I were as you;
If I could pray to move, prayers would move me:
But I am constant as the northern star,
Of whose true-fix'd and resting quality
There is no fellow in the firmament.
The skies are painted with unnumber'd sparks,
They are all fire and every one doth shine,
But there's but one in all doth hold his place.
(Act III, Scene i)

(i) What request has just been made of Caesar, and by whom? [3]
(ii) Explain the comparison Caesar draws between himself and the northern star. [3]
(iii) Who kneels next, and what happens immediately afterwards? [3]
(iv) Quote Caesar's dying words and explain their significance. [3]
(v) How does this speech contribute to the tragedy of the scene? [4]

[16 mark(s)] Source: Past Exam — ICSE 2026, Q2 and ICSE 2025 Improvement, Q2 (both set Act 3 Scene 1)

Q11. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

ANTONY:
Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears;
I come to bury Caesar, not to praise him.
The evil that men do lives after them;
The good is oft interred with their bones;
So let it be with Caesar. The noble Brutus
Hath told you Caesar was ambitious:
If it were so, it was a grievous fault,
And grievously hath Caesar answer'd it.
(Act III, Scene ii)

(i) Under what conditions has Brutus permitted Antony to speak? [3]
(ii) Why does Antony begin by saying he comes to bury Caesar and not to praise him? [3]
(iii) What proofs does Antony go on to offer that Caesar was not ambitious? [4]
(iv) Explain the effect of the repeated phrase 'and Brutus is an honourable man'. [3]
(v) What is the outcome of the oration, and what does it show about the crowd? [4]

[17 mark(s)] Source: Past Exam — ICSE 2025, Q2 (Act 3 Scene 2)

Q12. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

ANTONY: Post back with speed, and tell him what hath chanc'd:
Here is a mourning Rome, a dangerous Rome,
No Rome of safety for Octavius yet;
Hie hence, and tell him so. Yet, stay awhile;
Thou shalt not back till I have borne this corse
Into the market-place: there shall I try,
In my oration, how the people take
The cruel issue of these bloody men;
According to which, thou shalt discourse
To young Octavius of the state of things.
Lend me your hand.

(i) To whom is Antony speaking, and what instructions does he give? [3]
(ii) Why does Antony say Rome is 'dangerous'? For whom is it not safe, and why? [3]
(iii) Mention any three predictions Antony makes when he is left alone with Caesar's body. [3]
(iv) What were Brutus's and Cassius's opinions of Antony? Whose assessment proved correct? Justify your answer. [3]
(v) What aspects of Antony's character are revealed during Acts III and IV? [4]

Q13. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

ANTONY: You all do know this mantle: I remember
The first time ever Caesar put it on;
'Twas on a summer's evening, in his tent,
That day he overcame the Nervii.

(i) Who were the 'Nervii'? Why does Antony mention the names of Cassius, Casca and Brutus as he shows the mob the mantle? [3]
(ii) State three arguments Antony had put forward earlier to prove that Caesar was not ambitious. [3]
(iii) Why does Antony refer to Brutus as 'well-beloved', and why is the stabbing by Brutus called 'the most unkindest cut of all'? [3]
(iv) Mention three ways in which Brutus's speech differs from Antony's. [3]
(v) What characteristic of the mob is highlighted in this scene? State two traits of Antony revealed here and justify each with a reason. [4]

Q14. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

CAESAR: Are we all ready? What is now amiss
That Caesar and his Senate must redress?
METELLUS: Most high, most mighty, and most puissant Caesar,
Metellus Cimber throws before thy seat
A humble heart— [Kneels]

(i) Where are the speakers? What does 'puissant' mean? Explain: 'Metellus Cimber throws before thy seat a humble heart'. [3]
(ii) At the start of the scene Caesar says, 'The ides of March are come.' Why does he say this? What does the Soothsayer reply? [3]
(iii) What specific duties had the conspirators allotted to Trebonius and to Casca? Why does Cassius become nervous when Popilius Lena speaks to him as they enter? [3]
(iv) What does Artemidorus want of Caesar? How does Caesar respond to his plea? [3]
(v) Shortly after this exchange Caesar is stabbed to death. Whom do you sympathise with — Julius Caesar or the conspirators? Give reasons for your choice. [4]
