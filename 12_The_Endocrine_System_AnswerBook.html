D. Descriptive Type

Q72. (a) Endocrine system: consists of several endocrine glands/glandular cells which activate each other and work together as a system to bring about overall chemical coordination in the body. (b) Hormones: secretions from some glandular part of the body, which are poured directly into the blood and act on distant target organs/cells. (c) Myxoedema: a condition that occurs in adults due to hypothyroidism, in which the person becomes sluggish, with swelling of the face and hands.

Official Answer

Q73. (a) Exocrine glands secrete substances (enzymes, sweat, sebum, saliva, milk) into ducts, e.g. salivary gland; Endocrine glands secrete hormones directly into the blood, with no duct, e.g. Thyroid, Pituitary, Adrenal, Pancreas. (b) Enzymes act within cells/locally, not transported via blood, act on substrates not 'target organs'; Hormones are released into the blood and are transported to specific target organs, often far from the source gland. (c) Nervous control: transmission is electro-chemical along nerves, effect is rapid and short-lived, localised; Hormonal control: transmission is chemical via the blood, effect may be slow but is often widespread and longer-lasting. (d) Diabetes mellitus is caused by insufficient insulin, leading to high blood sugar and glucose in the urine; Diabetes insipidus is caused by insufficient ADH (Vasopressin), leading to excessive dilute urine with no glucose present. (e) Addison's disease is caused by hyposecretion of adrenal cortex hormones (cortisone), leading to low blood sugar (hypoglycemia); Cushing's syndrome is caused by hypersecretion of adrenal cortex hormones (cortisone), leading to high blood sugar (hyperglycemia).

Official Answer

Q74. (a) Adrenaline prepares the body to meet any emergency situation — for 'fight' or 'flight' — by providing extra energy and strength (increased heartbeat, raised blood glucose, etc.), so it is called the emergency hormone. (b) The pituitary is called the master gland because it controls the functioning of all the other endocrine glands, through its tropic hormones. (c) The soil (and hence food and water) of low Himalayan hilly regions is deficient in iodine, which is essential for thyroxine synthesis, so people there commonly suffer from (simple) goitre. (d) Iodised salt contains iodine, which is the active ingredient required for the production of thyroxine, so its regular use prevents iodine-deficiency (simple) goitre.

G. Think and Connect

Q89. Speed: Nervous response is immediate/rapid, while hormonal response is usually slower to take effect. Transmission: Nervous response is transmitted electro-chemically along nerve fibres/across synapses; hormonal response is transmitted chemically through the bloodstream. General nature of changes: Nervous response is short-lived and localised (affects specific muscles/glands), while hormonal response can be widespread throughout the body and may be short or long-lasting; hormones can also regulate growth and metabolism, which nerves cannot.

Official Answer

Q90. (1) Nerve impulses travel electro-chemically along nerve fibres and act almost instantly, while hormones travel chemically through the blood and generally act more slowly. (2) Nervous effects are usually short-lived and confined to specific muscles/glands, while hormonal effects are often widespread throughout the body and may last much longer. (3) Nervous responses cannot be modified by experience in the way behaviour can, and nerves do not regulate growth or long-term metabolism, whereas hormones (e.g. growth hormone, thyroxine) directly regulate growth and metabolic rate.
