C. Short Answer Type

Q35. Since RBCs lack a nucleus, the entire cell volume is free to be packed with haemoglobin, letting them carry more oxygen. Since they also lack mitochondria, they do not use up any of the oxygen they are carrying for their own respiration (they respire anaerobically), so all the oxygen picked up in the lungs is delivered to the body's tissues without loss. The absence of these organelles also gives the RBC its flexible, biconcave shape, allowing it to squeeze through narrow capillaries.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q36. A semi-permeable (selectively permeable) membrane is one that allows only certain substances (such as water, and a few solutes) to pass through it, while restricting the passage of others. In a plant cell, the cell membrane (plasma membrane) is the semi-permeable membrane; in the specific context of a mature plant cell's osmotic behaviour, the tonoplast (the membrane surrounding the large central vacuole) is the one that chiefly governs water movement.

D. Descriptive Type Questions

Q37. (a) Cell: the smallest structural and functional unit of a living organism, capable of carrying out all the basic processes of life. (b) Organelles: specialised, usually membrane-bound structures within the cytoplasm, each performing a specific function necessary for the cell's survival (e.g. mitochondria, Golgi body). (c) Cytoplasm: the jelly-like, semi-fluid substance filling the cell between the cell membrane and the nuclear membrane, in which the organelles are suspended and most metabolic reactions occur. (d) Protoplasm: the entire living content of a cell (cytoplasm plus nucleus together); often called the 'physical basis of life'. (e) Nucleus: a large, usually spherical, membrane-bound organelle that contains the chromatin/chromosomes (the cell's genetic material) and controls and regulates all the cell's activities.

Official Answer

Q38. (a) A plant cell has a rigid cellulose cell wall, a single large central vacuole, and plastids, and usually lacks a centrosome; an animal cell has no cell wall (only a cell membrane), small or no vacuoles, no plastids, and a centrosome. (b) The cell wall is the outermost, non-living, rigid, freely-permeable layer made of cellulose, found only in plant cells (and providing shape/support); the cell membrane is a living, flexible, selectively-permeable layer made of lipoproteins, found in every cell (plant and animal), and it regulates what enters/leaves the cell. (c) The centrosome is a cytoplasmic organelle near the nucleus, containing two centrioles, that organises the spindle fibres during cell division; a chromosome is a thread-like structure of DNA and histone proteins, located inside the nucleus, that becomes visible during cell division and carries the cell's genetic information (genes). (d) A chloroplast is a plastid containing chlorophyll, the site of photosynthesis, found in the green parts of a plant; a chromoplast is a plastid containing pigments other than chlorophyll (such as carotene), which gives colour to flowers and fruits, and does not carry out photosynthesis.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q39. (i) An organelle is usually a specialised structure enclosed by its own membrane (a few, like ribosomes and the centrosome, are non-membranous), suspended within the cytoplasm. (ii) Each organelle carries out one or more specific function(s) essential to the survival and normal functioning of the cell (e.g. mitochondria for respiration, the Golgi body for packaging and secretion). (iii) Some organelles are common to both plant and animal cells (e.g. mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, nucleus), while a few are unique to one type only (e.g. chloroplasts, cell wall and a large vacuole occur only in plant cells; the centrosome is mostly restricted to animal cells).

Q40. As a cell grows larger, its volume increases faster (as the cube of its linear dimension) than its surface area (which increases only as the square). This lowers the cell's surface-area-to-volume ratio, making it harder for the cell membrane to exchange nutrients, gases and wastes with the surroundings efficiently by diffusion, and for the nucleus to effectively control an increasingly large volume of cytoplasm. To keep this ratio favourable for efficient exchange and control, cells stay small and increase in number by dividing, rather than growing indefinitely in size.
