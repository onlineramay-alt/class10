A LOCUS is the path traced by a point moving under a stated condition.

THE THREE STANDARD LOCI IN THE SYLLABUS:
1. Points at a FIXED DISTANCE from a fixed point → a CIRCLE with that point as centre.
2. Points EQUIDISTANT FROM TWO FIXED POINTS → the PERPENDICULAR BISECTOR of the line joining them.
3. Points EQUIDISTANT FROM TWO INTERSECTING LINES → the pair of ANGLE BISECTORS of the angles between them.

Locus questions are answered with ruler and compasses, and the CONSTRUCTION ARCS MUST BE LEFT ON THE PAGE. Rubbing them out loses the marks even when the answer is right.

A. Worked Problems

Q1. (i) A CIRCLE of radius 4 cm with O as centre. (In three dimensions it would be a sphere, but the syllabus is confined to a plane.)

(ii) The PERPENDICULAR BISECTOR of the line segment AB — the line that cuts AB at right angles through its mid-point. Every point on it is equidistant from A and B, and no point off it is.

(iii) The pair of ANGLE BISECTORS of the angles formed where the two lines intersect. There are two such bisectors, and they are themselves perpendicular to each other.

These three account for nearly every locus question set, alone or in combination.

Q2. CONSTRUCTION, step by step. Leave every arc on the page.

STEP 1 — draw the triangle. Draw AB = 6 cm. With centre A and radius 4 cm draw an arc; with centre B and radius 5 cm draw another. They meet at C. Join AC and BC.

STEP 2 — the locus of points equidistant from AB and AC. Two intersecting lines, so the locus is the BISECTOR OF ANGLE A. With centre A and any convenient radius, draw an arc cutting AB at P and AC at Q. With centres P and Q and equal radii, draw arcs meeting at R. Join AR and produce it — that is the bisector.

STEP 3 — the locus of points equidistant from B and C. Two fixed points, so the locus is the PERPENDICULAR BISECTOR OF BC. With centre B and radius more than half BC, draw arcs above and below BC; repeat with centre C and the same radius; join the two intersections.

STEP 4 — mark P where the two loci cross.

WHAT P IS: it is equidistant from the lines AB and AC, and equidistant from the points B and C.

THE THREE THINGS EXAMINERS CHECK: that the measurements are accurate to within about 1 mm; that the construction arcs are still visible; and that each locus is described in words as well as drawn.

Q3. The locus is a PAIR OF LINES, each PARALLEL to l and each at a perpendicular distance of 3 cm from it — one on either side.

BOTH lines must be stated. A point at a fixed distance from a line can lie on either side of it, so the locus consists of two parallel lines, not one. Giving only one is the standard half-answer here.

(Compare the locus at a fixed distance from a POINT, which is a single circle — the difference is that a line has two sides and a point does not.)
