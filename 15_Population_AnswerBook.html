D. Descriptive Type

Q51. (a) Population — a group of individuals of the same species living and interbreeding within a given geographical area at a given time.
(b) Population density — the number of individuals of a population living per unit area (e.g. per square kilometre) at a given time.
(c) Demography — the statistical study of human population, specifically with reference to its size, density, distribution and vital statistics (births, deaths, etc.).
(d) Resources — any substance (natural or artificial), form of energy, or organism that is used by humans to meet their needs.
(e) Growth rate of population — the rate at which the number of individuals in a population increases (or decreases) over a given period of time; it becomes negative when mortality exceeds natality.

Official Answer

Q52. (a) Exhaustible resources — present in limited quantities and get used up/cannot be renewed within a human timescale (e.g. fossil fuels); Inexhaustible resources — present in unlimited/renewable quantities and do not get used up (e.g. solar energy, wind, tidal energy).
(b) Conventional sources — traditional, widely-used, mostly non-renewable and exhaustible (e.g. coal, petroleum); Non-conventional sources — newer, mostly renewable alternative sources (e.g. solar, wind, tidal energy).
(c) Natality (birth rate) — number of live births per 1000 people of population per year; Mortality (death rate) — number of deaths per 1000 people of population per year.
(d) Tubectomy — permanent surgical sterilization in FEMALES, involving cutting and ligating the fallopian tubes (oviducts); Vasectomy — permanent surgical sterilization in MALES, involving cutting and ligating the vas deferens (sperm ducts).
(e) Deforestation — the cutting down/clearing of forests, usually for land, timber or urbanisation; Afforestation — the planting of new trees/forests on previously non-forested land.

Official Answer

Q53. The 3Rs are: Reduce (minimise the use/consumption of resources and the generation of waste), Reuse (use items again for the same or a different purpose instead of discarding them), and Recycle (process used/waste materials to make new products) — together they help conserve natural resources and reduce pressure on the environment from a growing population.

Official Answer

Q54. (1) Food — food production, despite the Green Revolution, struggles to keep pace with the rising number of mouths to feed, leading to shortages/malnutrition in many regions. (2) Water — the demand for fresh drinking and irrigation water keeps rising while supply stays limited, causing water scarcity. (3) Housing and land — rapid population growth outpaces the construction of adequate housing, causing overcrowding and the growth of slums.

Q55. Knowledge of reproduction lets couples understand and use effective family-planning methods to limit family size. Examples: (1) Using barrier methods (condoms, diaphragms) or oral contraceptive pills to prevent fertilisation/ovulation. (2) Undergoing permanent surgical sterilization (vasectomy in men, tubectomy in women) once the desired family size has been reached, or using an IUD (e.g. Copper-T) for longer-term but reversible contraception.

Official Answer

Q56. "Population explosion" refers to the extremely rapid and massive rise in world population that has occurred over the last few hundred years (especially since the Industrial Revolution and the advances in healthcare of the 20th century), far outpacing earlier, slower rates of growth — with serious consequent effects such as poverty, unemployment, pollution and shortages of food, water, housing and other resources.

Official Answer

Q57. Demography is the statistical study of human population as a whole (its size, distribution, vital statistics, etc.), whereas Population density is specifically the number of individuals of that population living per unit area at a given time — density is one particular measurable statistic that demography studies.
