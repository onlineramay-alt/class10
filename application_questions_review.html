C1. An unknown element Z lies in Period 3 of the Modern Periodic Table. Its oxide has the formula Z₂O₃ and dissolves both in dilute hydrochloric acid and in sodium hydroxide solution.
(a) Identify Z and state its group number.
(b) What is the nature of the oxide Z₂O₃?
(c) Write its electronic configuration and state its valency.

Periodic Table · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Z is aluminium; Group 13.
(b) Amphoteric: it reacts with both an acid and a base.
(c) 2, 8, 3; valency 3 (as in Z₂O₃).

C2. Statement I: A sodium atom is larger than a magnesium atom.
Statement II: Across a period, the nuclear charge increases while electrons are added to the same shell.
(a) Both statements are true, and Statement II explains Statement I.
(b) Both statements are true, but Statement II does not explain Statement I.
(c) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false.
(d) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true.

Periodic Table · Statement I / II · [1 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Both statements are true, and Statement II explains Statement I.
(Mg has one more proton pulling the same M-shell electrons inward, so its radius is smaller.)

C3. A student tests two white solids, P and Q. P melts only above 800 °C and conducts electricity when molten but not as a solid. Q melts at 80 °C and does not conduct electricity in any state.
(a) Which solid is electrovalent and which is covalent?
(b) Explain why P conducts when molten but not when solid.
(c) Suggest which of the two is likely to dissolve in water and which in an organic solvent.

Chemical Bonding · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) P is electrovalent (ionic); Q is covalent.
(b) In solid P the ions are held in fixed positions in the crystal lattice; on melting, the ions become free to move and carry the current.
(c) P (ionic) dissolves in water, a polar solvent; Q (covalent) is likely to dissolve in an organic solvent such as kerosene.

C4. Ammonia gas reacts with hydrogen chloride to form ammonium chloride. How many lone pairs remain on the nitrogen atom in the ammonium ion, NH₄⁺, and what new bond is formed?
(a) One; covalent (b) None; coordinate (c) One; coordinate (d) None; ionic

Chemical Bonding · MCQ · [1 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(b) None; coordinate
(The nitrogen's lone pair is donated to H⁺, forming a coordinate bond, so no lone pair is left on N.)

C5. A farmer finds that the soil in his field is too acidic for his crop.
(a) Name a suitable substance he could add to the soil and give a balanced equation for its action on a soil acid (take nitric acid as an example).
(b) Why should he NOT use sodium hydroxide, even though it is a stronger base?

Acids, Bases and Salts · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Slaked lime, calcium hydroxide (or quicklime / powdered limestone).
Ca(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O
(b) Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkali: it is corrosive and, used in excess, would make the soil strongly alkaline and harm the crop. Being very soluble, it is also easily washed away, whereas lime acts slowly and gently.

C6. You are given lead carbonate powder, dilute nitric acid, dilute sulphuric acid and sodium sulphate solution.
(a) Outline how you would prepare a pure, dry sample of lead sulphate.
(b) Give the balanced equations for the reactions.
(c) Explain why lead carbonate is NOT treated directly with dilute sulphuric acid.

Acids, Bases and Salts · Application · [4 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Add lead carbonate to dilute nitric acid until effervescence stops, and filter off the excess carbonate to get lead nitrate solution. Add sodium sulphate solution: a white precipitate of lead sulphate forms. Filter it, wash it with distilled water and dry it between filter papers.
(b) PbCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Pb(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂
Pb(NO₃)₂ + Na₂SO₄ → PbSO₄↓ + 2NaNO₃
(c) Lead sulphate is insoluble. It would form a coating on the lead carbonate and stop the reaction almost at once.

State what she would observe in each case when sodium hydroxide is added, first in a small quantity and then in excess, and how this identifies each bottle.

C9. An antacid tablet of mass 0.5 g contains calcium carbonate as its only active ingredient. When the tablet reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid, 0.088 g of carbon dioxide is produced. Calculate the mass of calcium carbonate in the tablet and its percentage by mass. [Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16]

Mole Concept and Stoichiometry · Numerical · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
100 g CaCO₃ gives 44 g CO₂.
Mass of CaCO₃ = 0.088 × 100 / 44 = 0.2 g
Percentage = 0.2 / 0.5 × 100 = 40%

Mole Concept and Stoichiometry · Numerical · [3 mark(s)]

C12. 6.5 g of zinc granules react completely with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. Calculate the volume of hydrogen evolved at STP and the mass of zinc chloride formed. [Zn = 65, Cl = 35.5]

H₂ = 0.1 mol = 0.1 × 22.4 = 2.24 litres

C13. A student electrolyses copper(II) sulphate solution using copper electrodes for 30 minutes.
(a) State what happens to the mass of each electrode.
(b) Will the blue colour of the solution change? Give a reason.
(c) If the copper electrodes are replaced by platinum electrodes, state two changes the student would observe.

Electrolysis · Case-based · [4 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) The anode loses mass (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻); the cathode gains mass (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu).
(b) No. Copper ions removed at the cathode are replaced by the same number released at the anode, so [Cu²⁺] stays constant.
(c) With inert platinum electrodes: oxygen bubbles are given off at the anode, and the blue colour gradually fades (the solution becomes acidic) as Cu²⁺ ions are discharged and not replaced.

(a) 60 cm³ (b) 30 cm³ (c) 15 cm³ (d) 10 cm³

Electrolysis · MCQ · [1 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(c) 15 cm³
(2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂: hydrogen at the cathode and oxygen at the anode, in a 2 : 1 ratio by volume.)

C15. In an aluminium plant, the carbon anodes must be replaced at regular intervals, and cryolite is always added to the alumina.
(a) Explain why the anodes need replacing; give an equation.
(b) State two reasons for adding cryolite.

Metallurgy · Application · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Oxygen liberated at the anode reacts with the hot carbon and burns it away as carbon dioxide: C + O₂ → CO₂.
(b) Cryolite lowers the fusion temperature of alumina (from about 2050 °C to about 950 °C), saving energy, and it increases the electrical conductivity of the mixture. (Fluorspar is also added to increase fluidity.)

(a) What colour is the fountain, and why?
(b) Which property of hydrogen chloride does the experiment demonstrate?
(c) Why is hydrogen chloride dried with concentrated sulphuric acid and not with quicklime?

Study of Compounds A — Hydrogen Chloride · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

(a) Explain what happened, with a balanced equation.
(b) Why can't ammonia be dried with concentrated sulphuric acid?

Study of Compounds B — Ammonia · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

C19. A bottle of concentrated nitric acid left on a sunny laboratory shelf turns yellow. Concentrated nitric acid is, however, transported in aluminium tankers.
(a) Explain the yellow colour with a balanced equation.

Study of Compounds C — Nitric Acid · Application · [3 mark(s)]

4HNO₃ → 4NO₂ + 2H₂O + O₂
(b) In dark (amber) glass bottles, in a cool place.
(c) Concentrated nitric acid renders aluminium passive by forming a thin, protective oxide layer on its surface, which stops further reaction.

C20. When a few drops of concentrated sulphuric acid are added to sugar, the sugar swells up into a black, porous mass and steam is given off.
(a) Name the property of sulphuric acid shown, and write the equation.
(b) Why must concentrated sulphuric acid always be added to water, and never water to the acid?

Study of Compounds D — Sulphuric Acid · Case-based · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Dehydrating property: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ → 12C + 11H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄ removes the water).
(b) Dissolving the acid releases a large amount of heat. If water is added to the acid, the small amount of water boils at once and the acid spatters. Adding the acid slowly to a large volume of water lets the heat spread out safely.

Mathematics

M1. A dealer in Pune (Maharashtra) sells a laptop to a dealer in Chennai (Tamil Nadu) for ₹12,000. The Chennai dealer sells it to a customer in Chennai for ₹15,000. The rate of GST is 18%.
(a) Which type(s) of GST are charged on each sale?
(b) Calculate the tax paid by the Chennai dealer on his purchase.
(c) Calculate the net GST payable by the Chennai dealer, showing the CGST and SGST.

Goods and Services Tax (GST) · Case-based · [4 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(a) Pune → Chennai is inter-state: IGST. Chennai → Chennai is intra-state: CGST + SGST (9% each).
(b) IGST = 18% of 12,000 = ₹2,160 (this is his input tax credit).
(c) Output tax = CGST 1,350 + SGST 1,350 = ₹2,700.
Net GST payable = 2,700 − 2,160 = ₹540, i.e. CGST ₹270 and SGST ₹270.

M2. Riya wants to collect ₹52,000 in 2 years to buy a scooter. Her bank's recurring deposit account pays 8% p.a. simple interest. How much must she deposit every month, and how much interest will she earn?

Banking (Recurring Deposit Accounts) · Application · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

Let the monthly deposit be ₹P; n = 24.
Interest = P × (24 × 25)/2 × 8/(100 × 12) = 300P × 8/1200 = 2P
Maturity value = 24P + 2P = 26P = 52,000 → P = ₹2,000
Interest = 2P = ₹4,000

(a) Find the rate of return on each investment.
(b) Find the annual dividend he would get by investing the whole amount in each company.
(c) Which is the better investment?

Shares and Dividends · Application · [4 mark(s)]

(a) A: dividend per share ₹12 on ₹150 → 12/150 × 100 = 8%. B: dividend per share ₹5 on ₹40 → 5/40 × 100 = 12.5%.

(c) Company B (higher return).

M5. A rectangular garden needs 70 m of fencing all round and has an area of 300 m². Form a quadratic equation and find its length and breadth.

Quadratic Equations · Application · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

Length + breadth = 35. Let the length be x m; breadth = (35 − x) m.
x(35 − x) = 300 → x² − 35x + 300 = 0 → (x − 20)(x − 15) = 0
Length 20 m, breadth 15 m

M6. A train covers 300 km at a uniform speed. Had its speed been 10 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less. Find the original speed of the train.

Quadratic Equations · Application · [4 mark(s)]

Model Answer

Let the speed be x km/h. 300/x − 300/(x + 10) = 1
300(x + 10) − 300x = x(x + 10) → x² + 10x − 3000 = 0 → (x + 60)(x − 50) = 0
Speed cannot be negative: x = 50 km/h

Blue = 3 + 1 = 4 litres; white = 2 + 4 = 6 litres

M8. The volume of a box (in cm³) is given by V(x) = x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6, where its length, breadth and height are linear expressions in x.

(b) Find the volume of the box when x = 5.

(a) V(1) = 1 − 6 + 11 − 6 = 0, so (x − 1) is a factor.

(b) V(5) = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cm³

M16. Two straight roads cross at a junction O. A treasure is buried at a point which is equidistant from both roads and exactly 5 m from O.

M19. A drone hovers 60 m above a straight road. The angle of depression of a car moving towards the point directly below the drone changes from 30° to 60° in 4 seconds. Find the speed of the car. (Give your answer correct to 2 decimal places; √3 = 1.732)

M21. In a board game, two fair dice are thrown together and a player wins if the sum of the numbers is a prime number. Find the probability that the player (a) wins, and (b) does not win.

Probability · Application · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

Physics

P1. A force of 20 N applied at the handle of a door, 0.8 m from the hinges, just opens it. What force would be needed to open the door if it were pushed at a point 0.2 m from the hinges? Why are door handles fixed far from the hinges?

Force · Application · [2 mark(s)]

Model Answer

The moment needed is the same: 20 × 0.8 = F × 0.2 → F = 80 N.
A larger perpendicular distance gives the same moment with a smaller force, so handles are placed as far from the hinges as possible.

P2. A student checks the mass of a uniform metre rule without a balance. The rule balances horizontally on a knife-edge at the 35 cm mark when a 40 g mass hangs at the 5 cm mark. Find the mass of the metre rule, and state the principle used.

Force · Application · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

The weight of a uniform rule acts at its centre of gravity, the 50 cm mark.
Clockwise moment = M × (50 − 35) = 15M; anticlockwise moment = 40 × (35 − 5) = 1200
15M = 1200 → M = 80 g
Principle of moments: in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments.

P3. Assertion (A): When the string of a stone being whirled in a horizontal circle breaks, the stone flies off along the tangent to the circle.
Reason (R): At every instant in uniform circular motion, the velocity is directed along the tangent to the path.
(a) (A) is true and (R) is false.
(b) (A) is false and (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(d) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).

Force · Assertion–Reason · [1 mark(s)]

Model Answer

(c) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(Once the centripetal force (tension) disappears, the stone continues with the velocity it had, which is tangential.)

P4. A lift motor raises a loaded lift of total mass 500 kg through a height of 20 m in 25 s at a steady speed. (g = 10 m s⁻²)

P5. A roller-coaster car of mass 200 kg starts from rest at the top of a 45 m high slope. (g = 10 m s⁻²)

P8. A swimming pool looks only 1.5 m deep when seen from directly above. The refractive index of water is 4/3. What is its real depth, and why can this be dangerous for a non-swimmer?

P9. The refractive index of diamond is 2.42.

P21. 200 g of water at 80 °C is mixed with 300 g of water at 20 °C in an insulated flask of negligible heat capacity. Find the final temperature of the mixture, and state the principle used.

Calorimetry · Numerical · [3 mark(s)]

Model Answer

Heat lost = heat gained: 200 × c × (80 − t) = 300 × c × (t − 20)
16,000 − 200t = 300t − 6,000 → 500t = 22,000 → t = 44 °C
Principle of calorimetry (method of mixtures): heat lost by the hot body = heat gained by the cold body.

P22. Meera wants to cool 250 g of juice from 30 °C to 10 °C by adding ice cubes at 0 °C. Find the minimum mass of ice needed. [Specific heat capacity of juice and water = 4.2 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹; specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 336 J g⁻¹]
