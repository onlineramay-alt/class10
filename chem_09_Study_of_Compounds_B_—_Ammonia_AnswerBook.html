Chemistry — Study of Compounds B — Ammonia

Ammonia is the most heavily examined of the four compounds, because it carries both a laboratory preparation and an industrial one (Haber's process), and because the Fountain Experiment and the reaction with chlorine are perennial favourites.

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (b) Finely divided IRON, with molybdenum as a promoter. (Platinum is the catalyst in Ostwald's process for nitric acid, and vanadium pentoxide in the Contact process for sulphuric acid — the three are regularly confused and regularly tested against one another.)

Official Answer

Q2. (b) QUICKLIME (calcium oxide). Ammonia is a basic gas, so it cannot be dried over the acidic drying agents — concentrated sulphuric acid and phosphorus pentoxide would both react with it. Anhydrous calcium chloride is also unusable, because it combines with ammonia to form CaCl₂·8NH₃. Quicklime is the only common drying agent that is basic and therefore inert towards it.

B. Identify / Name / Very Short Answer

Q3. The process is HABER'S PROCESS.

Nitrogen and hydrogen, in the ratio 1 : 3 by volume, are combined at about 200 atmospheres pressure and 450–500 °C, over a catalyst of FINELY DIVIDED IRON with MOLYBDENUM as a promoter:

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ + heat

Official Answer

Q4. The mixture leaving the catalyst chamber is passed through a CONDENSER and COOLED. Ammonia has a much higher boiling point than nitrogen or hydrogen, so it LIQUEFIES and is run off, while the unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen remain gaseous and are RECIRCULATED back into the catalyst chamber.

Official Answer

Q5. AMMONIA, NH₃. It is the only common alkaline gas, which is why it alone turns moist red litmus blue, and the white fumes are ammonium chloride.

C. Short Answer Type

Q6. (i) That ammonia is EXTREMELY SOLUBLE IN WATER. The few drops of water introduced dissolve so much gas so quickly that a partial vacuum forms in the flask, and it is atmospheric pressure that then drives the water up the jet as a fountain. Without very high solubility there would be no fountain.

(ii) That its aqueous solution is ALKALINE (basic). The water in the trough is coloured with red litmus, and the fountain entering the flask turns BLUE.

(Compare hydrogen chloride, where the same experiment gives a red fountain from blue litmus — the solubility is demonstrated identically, the acid-base character oppositely.)

Official Answer

Q7. REACTANTS. A mixture of AMMONIUM CHLORIDE and SLAKED LIME (calcium hydroxide), heated:
2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O + 2NH₃↑

WHY THE TUBE IS SLOPED. The reaction produces water, which condenses on the cooler upper part of the hard-glass tube. If the mouth of the tube were not tilted DOWNWARD, that water would run back onto the hot glass and crack it. This is a standard examination point.

COLLECTION. By DOWNWARD DISPLACEMENT OF AIR — the gas jar is inverted and the delivery tube goes to the top of it — because ammonia is LIGHTER THAN AIR (relative molecular mass 17 against 29). It cannot be collected over water, being extremely soluble.

DRYING. Over QUICKLIME (calcium oxide) only, for the reasons given above.

Q8. (a) WITH AMMONIA IN EXCESS, the chlorine is completely reduced to hydrogen chloride, which at once combines with the surplus ammonia to give dense WHITE FUMES of ammonium chloride, and nitrogen is set free:
8NH₃ + 3Cl₂ → N₂ + 6NH₄Cl

(b) WITH CHLORINE IN EXCESS, there is no surplus ammonia to mop up the hydrogen chloride, and the highly explosive NITROGEN TRICHLORIDE is formed instead — an oily yellow liquid:
NH₃ + 3Cl₂ → NCl₃ + 3HCl

The contrast between the two is a favourite examination question, and the answer to the 'excess ammonia' form is the first equation.

Official Answer

Q9. (i) In the manufacture of NITROGENOUS FERTILIZERS — urea, ammonium sulphate, ammonium nitrate and ammonium phosphate — which is by far its largest use and the reason Haber's process transformed world agriculture.

(ii) In the manufacture of NITRIC ACID by Ostwald's process, and of sodium carbonate by the Solvay (ammonia-soda) process.

(iii) As a REFRIGERANT in ice plants and cold storage, because liquid ammonia has a high latent heat of vaporisation and absorbs a great deal of heat on evaporating.

(Also acceptable: in the manufacture of explosives, rayon and nylon, and as a household cleaning agent, since it removes grease.)

D. Structured / Section B Questions

Q10. (a) HABER'S PROCESS.

(b) FINELY DIVIDED IRON, with molybdenum as a promoter.

(c) The gases leaving the catalyst chamber are passed through a CONDENSER and cooled. Ammonia, having a much higher boiling point than nitrogen or hydrogen, LIQUEFIES and is drawn off; the unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen stay gaseous and are RECIRCULATED to the catalyst chamber.

(d) That ammonia is EXTREMELY SOLUBLE in water — the dissolving of the gas creates the vacuum that produces the fountain; and that its solution is ALKALINE — the red litmus in the water turns blue.

CONDITIONS worth adding if the question asks for them: about 200 atmospheres pressure and 450–500 °C. The reaction N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ is exothermic and proceeds with a decrease in volume, so by Le Chatelier's principle high pressure favours the yield while a low temperature would too — but too low a temperature makes the reaction impracticably slow, so a moderate temperature is used as a compromise and the catalyst supplies the rate.
