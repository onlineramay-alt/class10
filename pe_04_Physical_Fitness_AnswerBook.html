B. Very Short Answer

Q3. Physical fitness is the ability of a person to carry out their daily tasks efficiently and without undue fatigue, with enough energy left in reserve to meet emergencies and to enjoy leisure.

The two halves of the definition both matter: the work of the day done WITHOUT UNDUE FATIGUE, and something left over afterwards. A person exhausted by their ordinary duties is not physically fit however strong they may be.

Q4. STRENGTH is the maximum force a muscle or group of muscles can exert in a single effort — a weightlifter's one heavy lift. ENDURANCE is the ability to sustain an activity over a long period, resisting fatigue — a marathon runner's hours of work at a lower intensity. Strength is about MAGNITUDE, endurance about DURATION.

SPEED is the ability to move the body or a part of it from one point to another in the shortest possible time, generally in a STRAIGHT line — a 100 m sprinter. AGILITY is the ability to CHANGE direction and position of the body quickly and under control — a badminton player at the net. Speed is about how fast, agility about how quickly you can change.

C. Short Answer Type

Q5. (i) CARDIOVASCULAR / RESPIRATORY ENDURANCE — the ability of the heart, blood vessels and lungs to supply oxygen to the working muscles over a sustained period. Example: a long-distance runner or a swimmer over 1500 m.

(ii) STRENGTH — the maximum force a muscle group can exert against a resistance in a single effort. Example: a weightlifter, or a wrestler holding an opponent.

(iii) FLEXIBILITY — the range of movement possible at a joint. Example: a gymnast performing a split, or a hurdler clearing the barrier.

(iv) POWER — the ability to exert maximum force in the shortest possible time; strength multiplied by speed. Example: a shot-putter, a high jumper at take-off, or a batsman driving for six.

(v) SPEED — the ability to move the whole body or a part of it from one point to another in the least time. Example: a 100 m sprinter, or a fast bowler's arm action.

(vi) AGILITY — the ability to change the position and direction of the body rapidly and under control. Example: a badminton player covering the court, or a footballer dribbling through defenders.

(The remaining four in the syllabus: STAMINA, the capacity to sustain prolonged effort; COORDINATION, the harmonious working together of eye, hand and foot, as in a batsman's stroke; BALANCE, the ability to maintain equilibrium whether still or moving, as on a gymnastics beam; and ACCURACY, the ability to direct a movement to a target, as in archery or a penalty stroke.)

Q6. (i) REGULAR EXERCISE. The single most important factor. Fitness is developed only by working the body regularly and progressively, and it is LOST when training stops — the principle of reversibility. Neither occasional violent effort nor a former athlete's past training will maintain it.

(ii) DIET AND NUTRITION. A balanced diet supplies the material for tissue repair and the energy for work. Undernutrition brings weakness and fatigue; overnutrition brings obesity, which loads the heart and joints and reduces every component of fitness.

(iii) REST AND SLEEP. The body adapts and repairs during recovery, not during the training itself. Inadequate sleep prevents that adaptation, so that training produces staleness and overuse injury rather than improvement.

(iv) AGE AND GENDER. Fitness rises through youth, peaks in early adulthood and declines gradually thereafter, so the standard expected must be appropriate to the age. Men and women differ in muscle mass and in cardiovascular capacity, and reach their peaks at somewhat different ages.

(v) HEREDITY, and the closely related factor of BODY TYPE. Inherited characteristics fix the potential for height, build, muscle-fibre composition and lung capacity, so two people training identically will not reach identical fitness.

(Other acceptable factors: environment and climate; illness, injury and disease; personal habits such as smoking and alcohol; posture; and the individual's own motivation and attitude, without which no programme is followed.)
