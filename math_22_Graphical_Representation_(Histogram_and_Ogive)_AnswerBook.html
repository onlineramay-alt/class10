This chapter is done on GRAPH PAPER, and the marks are for the graph. Two rules decide most of them:

STATE THE SCALE on both axes, in the form '1 cm = 10 units'. A graph without a stated scale loses marks however accurate it is.

USE CONTINUOUS CLASS INTERVALS. If the classes are given as 1–10, 11–20, 21–30 they are DISCONTINUOUS and must first be converted to 0.5–10.5, 10.5–20.5, 20.5–30.5 by subtracting 0.5 from each lower limit and adding 0.5 to each upper limit. A histogram cannot have gaps between its bars.

HISTOGRAM — bars, used to estimate the MODE.
OGIVE (cumulative frequency curve) — a smooth curve, used to estimate the MEDIAN, quartiles and percentiles.

A. Worked Problems

Q1. DRAWING THE HISTOGRAM.
1. The classes are already continuous (0–10, 10–20, …), so no adjustment is needed.
2. Choose and STATE the scales — for example, on the x-axis 1 cm = 10 units, on the y-axis 1 cm = 2 units.
3. Draw bars of heights 5, 8, 12, 7 and 3 over their classes, with NO GAPS between them.

ESTIMATING THE MODE FROM THE HISTOGRAM.
The modal class is the tallest bar, here 20–30 with frequency 12.

From the TOP-LEFT corner of the tallest bar, draw a straight line to the TOP-RIGHT corner of the bar immediately to its LEFT (the 10–20 bar).
From the TOP-RIGHT corner of the tallest bar, draw a straight line to the TOP-LEFT corner of the bar immediately to its RIGHT (the 30–40 bar).
The two lines cross at a point inside the tallest bar. Drop a PERPENDICULAR from that point to the x-axis, and read off the value.

For this data the mode is approximately **25**.

THE MARKS ARE FOR: the stated scale, bars of the right heights with no gaps, the two construction lines actually drawn, the perpendicular dropped, and the value read off and written down. Leaving out the construction lines and simply writing '25' scores almost nothing.

Q2. STEP 1 — build the CUMULATIVE FREQUENCY table.

Class | f | cf
0–10 | 5 | 5
10–20 | 8 | 13
20–30 | 12 | 25
30–40 | 7 | 32
40–50 | 3 | 35

N = 35

STEP 2 — plot the points. An ogive plots the cumulative frequency against the UPPER CLASS BOUNDARY of each class — not the class mark, and not the lower boundary. So plot:
(10, 5), (20, 13), (30, 25), (40, 32), (50, 35)

Also plot the starting point (0, 0), the lower boundary of the first class with zero cumulative frequency, so that the curve begins on the axis.

STEP 3 — join the points with a SMOOTH FREEHAND CURVE, not straight line segments. State the scales.

STEP 4 — read off the MEDIAN.
The median corresponds to the (N/2)th value = 35/2 = 17.5.
Draw a horizontal line from 17.5 on the cumulative frequency axis until it meets the curve; from that point drop a perpendicular to the x-axis and read the value.

For this data the median is approximately **23.75**.

SHOW BOTH LINES on the graph — the horizontal from the y-axis and the perpendicular to the x-axis. They are the evidence that the value was read from the curve, and marks are given for them.

THE SAME METHOD gives the QUARTILES: the lower quartile Q₁ at N/4 = 8.75 and the upper quartile Q₃ at 3N/4 = 26.25, read off in exactly the same way. The interquartile range is Q₃ − Q₁.
