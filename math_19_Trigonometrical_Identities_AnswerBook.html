THE THREE FUNDAMENTAL IDENTITIES:
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

STANDARD VALUES worth knowing cold:
0° 30° 45° 60° 90°
sin 0 1/2 1/√2 √3/2 1
cos 1 √3/2 1/√2 1/2 0
tan 0 1/√3 1 √3 undefined

METHOD FOR PROVING AN IDENTITY: work on ONE SIDE ONLY — usually the more complicated — and transform it until it equals the other. Never move terms across the equals sign as though it were an equation, because that assumes what you are trying to prove.

A. Worked Problems

Q1. Take the LEFT-HAND SIDE and transform it.

LHS = (1 − cos²θ)(1 + cot²θ)

Use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, so 1 − cos²θ = sin²θ.
Use 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.

LHS = sin²θ × cosec²θ

Since cosec θ = 1/sin θ, we have cosec²θ = 1/sin²θ:

LHS = sin²θ × (1/sin²θ)
= 1
= RHS ∎

Note that only the left side was touched. Writing 'LHS = RHS' at the start and manipulating both is a logical error, and examiners penalise it.

Q2. PROOF. Take the left side; it is a difference of two squares.

LHS = (sec θ − tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ)
= sec²θ − tan²θ

From the identity 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, we get sec²θ − tan²θ = 1.

LHS = 1 = RHS ∎

HENCE FIND sec θ − tan θ when sec θ + tan θ = 3.

From what we have just proved, the product of the two expressions is always 1:
(sec θ − tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ) = 1
(sec θ − tan θ)(3) = 1
**sec θ − tan θ = 1/3**

The word 'hence' means the second part MUST be answered using the first — that is where the marks are. Solving from scratch for θ would not earn them.

AS A BONUS: adding and subtracting the two results gives 2 sec θ = 3 + 1/3 = 10/3, so sec θ = 5/3; and 2 tan θ = 3 − 1/3 = 8/3, so tan θ = 4/3. Check: sec²θ − tan²θ = 25/9 − 16/9 = 1 ✓

Q3. Substitute the standard values.

sin 30° = 1/2
cos 60° = 1/2
cos 30° = √3/2
sin 60° = √3/2

= (1/2 × 1/2) + (√3/2 × √3/2)
= 1/4 + 3/4
= 4/4
= **1**

NOTE the pattern: this expression is sin(30° + 60°) = sin 90° = 1, by the compound-angle formula. That formula is not in the ICSE syllabus, but recognising the structure is a useful check on the arithmetic.

Also worth noticing: sin 30° = cos 60° and cos 30° = sin 60°, because sin θ = cos(90° − θ). That relation halves the number of values you need to remember.
