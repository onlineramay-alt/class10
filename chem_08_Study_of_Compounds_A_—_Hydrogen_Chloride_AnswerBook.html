Q2. SODIUM CHLORIDE (common salt) and CONCENTRATED SULPHURIC ACID, heated to BELOW 200 °C.

NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl↑ (below 200 °C)

The temperature is kept below 200 °C deliberately. Above that, the sodium hydrogen sulphate reacts with more sodium chloride to give sodium sulphate — 2NaCl + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2HCl — but that reaction needs a much higher temperature, at which the glass apparatus would be attacked and the sodium sulphate formed would set as a hard crust in the flask, difficult to remove.

Q3. Because concentrated sulphuric acid is a NON-VOLATILE acid with a high boiling point, while hydrogen chloride is VOLATILE. On heating, the less volatile acid displaces the more volatile one from its salt, and the HCl escapes as a gas, driving the reaction forward.

Nitric acid could not be used because it is itself volatile, so it would distil over with the product and contaminate it; and it is an oxidising agent, which would oxidise the hydrogen chloride to chlorine. Hydrochloric acid obviously cannot be used to prepare itself.

Q4. (i) It turns moist blue litmus paper RED, showing it is acidic. (It has no action on dry litmus, because water is needed for it to ionise.)

(ii) A glass rod dipped in AMMONIA SOLUTION and held in the gas gives DENSE WHITE FUMES of ammonium chloride:
NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl
This is the confirmatory test.

(iii) The gas fumes strongly in moist air, and gives a white precipitate of silver chloride, soluble in ammonium hydroxide, when passed into silver nitrate solution.

C. Short Answer Type

Q5. A dry round-bottomed flask is filled with hydrogen chloride gas and fitted with a stopper carrying a jet tube and a dropper. The jet is dipped into a trough of water coloured blue with litmus. A few drops of water are introduced from the dropper.

The gas dissolves almost instantly in those few drops, creating a partial VACUUM in the flask. The atmospheric pressure on the trough then forces the water up the jet, and it enters the flask as a FOUNTAIN, turning RED as it goes because the solution is acidic.

THE TWO PROPERTIES DEMONSTRATED:
(i) that hydrogen chloride is EXTREMELY SOLUBLE in water — the fountain only forms because the gas dissolves fast enough to create the vacuum;
(ii) that its solution is ACIDIC — shown by the blue litmus turning red.

Q6. It is collected by UPWARD DISPLACEMENT OF AIR — that is, downward delivery into a gas jar, the gas being led to the bottom — because hydrogen chloride is DENSER THAN AIR (relative molecular mass 36.5 against air's 29), so it sinks and displaces the air upwards out of the jar.

It cannot be collected OVER WATER because it is EXTREMELY SOLUBLE in water — about 450 volumes dissolve in one volume of water — so it would simply dissolve instead of being collected.

Q7. AQUA REGIA — 'royal water' — is a mixture of CONCENTRATED HYDROCHLORIC ACID and CONCENTRATED NITRIC ACID in the ratio 3 : 1 by volume.

Its importance is that it dissolves the NOBLE METALS, GOLD and PLATINUM, which neither acid can attack alone. The nitric acid oxidises the metal while the hydrochloric acid supplies chloride ions that form a stable soluble complex with it, and the two together achieve what neither can separately.

It is used in the refining and testing of gold and platinum, and in analytical chemistry to dissolve otherwise resistant samples.
