Q4. (i) In the manufacture of STEEL. Manganese is added to molten iron to remove oxygen and sulphur, and it is an essential ingredient of ferro-manganese alloy, which makes steel harder, tougher and resistant to wear. About ten kilograms of manganese are needed for every tonne of steel produced, and there is no substitute for it.

(ii) In the CHEMICAL AND ALLIED INDUSTRIES — in the manufacture of dry cell batteries, bleaching powder, insecticides, paints, glass and pottery, where manganese dioxide is used as an oxidising agent and as a colouring and decolourising material.

Official Answer

Q5. USES. Iron ore is smelted to produce pig iron and steel, which are the foundation of all modern industry — the manufacture of machinery and machine tools, railway lines, rolling stock, ships, motor vehicles, bridges, buildings, pipes, agricultural implements, and countless consumer goods. Steel production is taken as the index of a country's industrial development, which is why iron ore is called the backbone of industry.

TYPES. Magnetite is the finest, with 70 per cent metal content; haematite is the most abundant and commercially the most important, with 60 to 70 per cent; limonite and siderite are inferior, with much lower content.

CHIEF PRODUCING AREAS. Odisha — Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar and Sundargarh — is the largest producer. Jharkhand — Singhbhum, the area named in the syllabus. Chhattisgarh — Bailadila and Dalli-Rajhara, from which high-grade ore is exported through Visakhapatnam to Japan. Karnataka — Bellary, Hospet and Kudremukh. Goa, whose ore is largely exported. Also Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.

India has among the largest reserves of haematite in the world, and exports a substantial part of its output.

Q6. COPPER. Uses — because it is an excellent conductor of electricity and heat, it is used above all for electrical wiring, cables, generators, motors, transformers and telecommunications; also for utensils, coins, and for alloys such as brass (with zinc) and bronze (with tin). Producing areas — Khetri in Rajasthan, the Singhbhum belt in Jharkhand, and Balaghat and Malanjkhand in Madhya Pradesh. India's reserves are small and much copper must be imported.

BAUXITE. Uses — it is the ore of aluminium, and aluminium is light, strong, corrosion-resistant and a good conductor, so it is used in the aircraft and automobile industries, in electrical transmission lines, in utensils, in packaging and foil, and in construction. Bauxite is also used to make refractory bricks, abrasives and cement. Producing areas — Odisha, which is much the largest producer (Kalahandi, Koraput, Sambalpur), Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh. India's reserves are large.

Q7. METALLIC minerals contain metal in the raw state and can be melted to obtain new products; they are usually hard, have a shine or lustre, and are found in igneous and metamorphic rocks. Examples: iron ore, manganese, copper, bauxite, gold.

NON-METALLIC minerals contain no metal, are generally softer and without lustre, and are found chiefly in sedimentary rocks. Examples: limestone, mica, gypsum, coal, petroleum, salt.

Within the metallic group:

FERROUS minerals contain iron and are used in the iron and steel industry. Examples: iron ore, manganese, chromite, nickel, cobalt.

NON-FERROUS minerals contain no iron. Examples: copper, bauxite, lead, zinc, tin, gold, silver.

India is comparatively rich in ferrous minerals and poor in non-ferrous ones, which is why copper, lead, zinc and tin have largely to be imported.
