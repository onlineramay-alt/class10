A. Very Short Answer

Q1. Physical Education is that part of the general education process which aims at the all-round development of the individual — physical, mental, social and emotional — through carefully selected PHYSICAL ACTIVITIES conducted under qualified supervision.

The key words are 'part of education' and 'through physical activity'. It is not merely games or drill: the activity is the MEANS, and the development of the whole person is the END.

B. Short Answer Type

Q3. PHYSICAL DEVELOPMENT. To build and maintain a sound, efficient body: to develop the components of fitness — strength, speed, endurance, flexibility, agility, balance and coordination; to improve the working of the circulatory, respiratory, digestive, muscular and nervous systems; to correct postural defects; to develop neuromuscular skill so that movement becomes economical and graceful; and to establish habits of exercise, rest and hygiene that will keep the body sound in later life.

PSYCHOLOGICAL (MENTAL) DEVELOPMENT. To develop the mental qualities that activity demands and rewards: alertness, concentration, quick decision under pressure, judgement of speed and distance, tactical thinking and problem-solving. Games require a player to read a situation and act at once, which trains the mind as directly as it trains the body. Physical activity also relieves mental fatigue and stress, and improves sleep and consequently learning.

SOCIAL DEVELOPMENT. To make a person fit to live with others. Team games teach cooperation, the subordination of personal glory to the side, respect for the rules and for the official's decision, leadership when one is captain and followership when one is not, tolerance, fair play and courtesy to opponents. They also break down barriers of caste, class, religion and language, because on a field a person is judged by what they can do.

EMOTIONAL DEVELOPMENT. To develop control of the emotions and stability of temperament. Sport provides a legitimate outlet for aggression and surplus energy; it teaches a player to accept defeat without bitterness and victory without arrogance, to control anger at a bad decision, and to persist through fatigue and discouragement. Success in an activity builds self-confidence and self-esteem, which carry over into every other part of life.

THE UNDERLYING POINT, worth stating in a long answer: these four are not separate aims pursued by separate activities. A single game of hockey develops all four at once, which is why physical education uses games rather than exercises alone.

Q4. PHYSICAL EDUCATION is the broadest of the three. It is education THROUGH physical activity, aiming at the all-round development of the individual — physical, mental, social and emotional. It is compulsory, it is for everybody regardless of ability, and its purpose is the growth of the person.

PHYSICAL TRAINING is narrower and more mechanical. It is the systematic development of the body through drill and exercise, aiming chiefly at physical fitness, strength and discipline. It was the older military model, in which the participant obeys rather than learns, and the aim is the body rather than the whole person.

SPORTS are organised competitive physical activities governed by rules, in which the object is to WIN. Participation is voluntary and generally selective — the best are chosen — and the emphasis is on performance and excellence rather than on universal development.

The relation between them: physical education USES sports and training as its means, but its aims are wider than either.
