English Language — Notice and E-mail (Question 3)

ICSE Class 10 Answer Book

Generated 2026-09-01

This question was introduced in 2019 — the 2017 and 2018 papers have no Notice or E-mail question at all, and go straight from the letter to the comprehension. The two parts always share one situation: the notice announces the event, the e-mail does something about it (invites a judge, requests permission, asks a neighbouring school to send a team). Answer both from the same set of facts, and make sure the date, time and venue agree between them — examiners check.

A. Notice Writing

Q1. OFFICIAL — the mark scheme accepted BOTH of the following, which is itself the most useful thing to know about this question.

FORMAT 1 (fuller sentences)

Words Worth!
Annual Inter House Poetry Writing Festival
on 30th September 2025
from 10.00 a.m. to 12 noon
at Millennium Auditorium, Bright Academy

Pupils from Classes IX to XII who wish to participate must register their names with the Vice Principal on or before 18th September 2025.

FORMAT 2 (telegraphic, without the linking prepositions)

Words Worth!
Annual Inter House Poetry Writing Festival
30th September 2025
10.00 a.m. to 12 noon
Millennium Auditorium, Bright Academy

Pupils from Classes IX to XII who wish to participate must register their names with the Vice Principal on or by 18th September 2025.

WHAT THIS TELLS YOU: the marks are for the information, not for a rigid house style. Both versions carry the same five things — a title, the event, the date, the time, the venue, and the action required with its deadline. Either phrasing scores.

NOTICE CHECKLIST — the five questions every notice must answer
1. WHAT is happening (a heading, and a name for the event).
2. WHEN — date and time, both.
3. WHERE — the exact venue.
4. WHO it concerns — which classes or houses.
5. WHAT the reader must now DO — register, report, sign up — and by when, with whom.

Also: keep it inside a box; keep it short — a notice is not a paragraph; write in the third person and never in the first; and sign off with your name and designation (Head Boy / Secretary, Literary Club) at the bottom, with the date of issue at the top left. Do not write 'Dear students' — that is a letter, not a notice.

Official Answer

Q2. MODEL

BRIGHT ACADEMY
NOTICE

5 February 2026

INTER HOUSE NON-FLAME COOKING COMPETITION

The Home Science Department will hold an Inter House Non-Flame Cooking Competition on Saturday, 21 February 2026, from 9.00 a.m. to 11.30 a.m. in the School Cafeteria.

Each House may enter one team of two students from Classes IX and X. No form of flame or heating appliance may be used. Ingredients and utensils are to be brought by the participants. Dishes will be judged on taste, presentation, hygiene and originality.

Names must be submitted to the House Captains on or before 14 February 2026.

Rohit Menon
Secretary, Home Science Club

WHY THIS ANSWERS THE QUESTION: 'Non-Flame' is the distinguishing feature of this event, so a notice that does not say no flame may be used has missed the point of the topic. Whenever the event has an unusual condition in its name, that condition belongs in the notice.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q3. PLAN

An inter-school event needs one thing an in-house event does not: the notice must make clear who represents the school, since the whole school cannot enter.

Include: the name of the competition and that it is inter-school; date, time and venue; which classes may try out; the topic or theme if there is one, and the word limit and time limit; the selection process — that a school team of (say) three will be chosen from those who register; the deadline and the teacher with whom to register; and the prize, if there is one.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q4. PLAN

Note the two features that make this different from a school notice, and that carry the marks.

First, the audience. It is going up in a supermarket, so it is addressed to the general public of the locality, not to students. It must therefore identify the club, since readers will not know it, and it must explain what the programme is rather than assuming knowledge.

Second, the purpose. The question says it is 'to create awareness', so it is an invitation as much as an announcement — it should tell people they are welcome to attend, and give them a reason to come.

Include: the club's name; the event and its occasion; date, time and venue; a line on what the programme will contain (songs, a skit, felicitation of teachers of the locality); who may attend and whether entry is free; a contact person and telephone number; and your name and designation as President.

WATCH: a notice for a public place should carry a contact number. One meant for a school notice board usually does not. Adjust to the audience the question specifies — that judgement is exactly what is being marked.

B. E-mail Writing

Q5. OFFICIAL — as printed in the mark scheme

To: joygoswami@gmail.com

Subject: Annual Inter House Poetry Writing Festival

Dear Sir,

Our school is hosting an Inter House Poetry Writing Festival to celebrate our Golden Jubilee. We would be honoured if you would be our Chief Guest for the occasion and inaugurate the festival.

The festival will be held on 30th September 2025 from 10.00 a.m. to 12 noon at the Millennium Auditorium of our school, Bright Academy. Pupils from Classes IX to XII will be participating.

We look forward to your presence with us. A line in confirmation would be much appreciated.

Thanking you,

Yours faithfully,
Mahinderpal Singh

STUDY THE SHAPE — it is only three short paragraphs, and each does one job: why I am writing; the particulars; the request for a reply. Note also that every fact in it matches the notice for the same event. That agreement between the two halves of Question 3 is deliberately checked.

E-MAIL CHECKLIST
1. 'To:' with an address. 2. 'Subject:' — compulsory, and it should name the event, not say 'Invitation'. 3. Salutation as in a formal letter. 4. Body: purpose, details, request. 5. 'Yours faithfully' and your name. No address block and no date — the medium supplies both, and candidates who paste a letter's heading into an e-mail lose format marks. Keep it shorter than a letter; that brevity is the point of the form.

Official Answer

Q6. MODEL

To: chef.anahitadesai@gmail.com

Subject: Invitation to judge the Inter House Non-Flame Cooking Competition, 21 February 2026

Madam,

Bright Academy, Dhanbad, is holding its Inter House Non-Flame Cooking Competition on Saturday, 21 February 2026, and we should be honoured if you would consent to judge it.

The competition will be held from 9.00 a.m. to 11.30 a.m. in the school cafeteria. Eight teams of two students each, from Classes IX and X, will prepare dishes without the use of any flame or heating appliance, and will be judged on taste, presentation, hygiene and originality. We expect the judging to take about forty minutes.

We know how much your time is in demand, and we would gladly arrange transport at your convenience. A line in confirmation by 14 February would help us complete our arrangements.

Thank you.

Yours faithfully,
Rohit Menon
Secretary, Home Science Club
Bright Academy

NOTE how the details agree exactly with the notice for the same event in the previous section — same date, same time, same venue, same classes. Writing the notice first and then drawing the e-mail's facts from it is the reliable way to avoid contradicting yourself under examination pressure.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q7. PLAN

The most frequently set version of this question — it appears in four papers on record — so it is worth having ready.

Subject line: name the competition and the date.

Paragraph 1 — who you are, which school, and what is being held.

Paragraph 2 — the particulars the other school actually needs in order to decide: date, time, venue, the number of students per team, the classes eligible, the format of the competition, and the last date for confirming participation. This is the paragraph candidates under-write, and it is where most of the five marks sit.

Paragraph 3 — the request itself, and an offer: that you will send the detailed rules on confirmation, and that refreshments and, if relevant, transport will be arranged.

WATCH: this e-mail is from a student, so it must be written on the school's behalf and signed with a designation — Secretary of the Literary Society, Head Boy, Cultural Captain. An unsigned or purely personal e-mail to another school's Principal is out of register, and the register is being marked.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q8. PLAN

This one is a request to an outside institution for a favour of a fairly specific kind, so it must justify itself.

Subject: Request for a resource person — Mask-Making Event, [date].

Paragraph 1 — introduce the school and the event: an Inter House creative mask-making competition using renewable materials, organised by the Science Club, on a stated date.

Paragraph 2 — the request, and precisely what is being asked of the doctor: a talk of about twenty minutes to roughly sixty students, on what makes a mask effective — material, fit, layers, and the difference between decorative and protective masks — followed by questions.

Paragraph 3 — why it matters, briefly: the students will be making masks and should understand what actually protects, so that the event teaches something beyond craft.

Paragraph 4 — the practical arrangements: date, time, venue, that the school will arrange transport, and a request for confirmation.

WATCH: you are asking a busy professional for their time, so state clearly how much of it you need. An invitation that does not say how long the speaker will be required is the commonest fault in this kind of e-mail, and a good answer avoids it.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q9. PLAN

Two distinct requests in one e-mail — permission and sponsorship — and both must be made clearly. Candidates routinely make the first and forget the second.

Subject: Request for permission to display a notice, and for sponsorship — Teachers' Day programme.

Paragraph 1 — who you are: the President of a named children's club in the locality, and what the club is planning.

Paragraph 2 — request one: permission to display a notice on the supermarket's board, with the reason that it is where the residents of the locality will see it. Say how large the notice is and how long you would like it displayed.

Paragraph 3 — request two: sponsorship. Be specific about what you are asking for — a sum, or refreshments, or printing — and be equally specific about what the supermarket receives: its name on the notice and the banner, an acknowledgement at the programme, goodwill in the neighbourhood it serves.

Paragraph 4 — an offer to call on him with the details, and a request for a reply.

WATCH: when asking for money, the mark of a good request is that it explains the benefit to the giver. That is a judgement the examiner is looking for.
