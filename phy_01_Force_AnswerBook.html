Q84. (a) Both forces at the same point: The two forces are equal and opposite, so the net force = F − F = 0. The net torque = F × 0 = 0 (both forces act through the same point, so perpendicular distance = 0). The body remains in equilibrium — no translational or rotational motion.

Q85. A couple is a pair of two equal, opposite, and parallel forces whose lines of action do not coincide. Effect: A couple produces a turning (rotational) effect on a body without causing any translational motion. The net force of a couple is zero, but the net torque is non-zero.
Examples from daily life:
(i) Turning a water tap — both hands apply equal and opposite forces on the two handles of the tap.
(ii) Opening or closing the cap of a bottle — fingers apply equal and opposite forces on opposite sides of the cap.
(iii) Winding a watch — the two fingers apply a couple to wind the key.

Official Answer

Q86. Proof:
Let two equal and opposite forces, each of magnitude F, act at points A and B respectively (AB = d, the couple arm). Let O be any point on the line AB at distance x from A.
Moment of force F at A about O = F × OA = F × x (anticlockwise)
Moment of force F at B about O = F × OB = F × (d − x) (anticlockwise)
Total moment = F·x + F·(d − x) = F·x + F·d − F·x = F·d
Thus, Moment of couple = F × d = Force × couple arm.
Note: The moment is independent of the position of point O, confirming that the moment of a couple is constant regardless of the reference point chosen.

Official Answer

Q87. Experiment to verify the Principle of Moments:
Apparatus: metre rule, knife-edge (fulcrum) at the 50 cm mark, two sets of slotted weights with hangers, two spring balances.
Procedure:
1. Balance the metre rule horizontally on the knife-edge at the 50 cm mark.
2. Hang weight W₁ (e.g., 200 gf) on the left side at distance d₁ (e.g., 30 cm from the fulcrum).
3. Hang weight W₂ on the right side at distance d₂ and adjust until the rule is again horizontal.
4. Record d₂ and the value of W₂.
5. Calculate: Anticlockwise moment = W₁ × d₁; Clockwise moment = W₂ × d₂.
6. Repeat with different weights and positions.
Observation: In each case, W₁ × d₁ = W₂ × d₂.
Conclusion: The sum of anticlockwise moments equals the sum of clockwise moments, verifying the Principle of Moments.
