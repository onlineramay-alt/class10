C. Short Answer Type

Q18. He must be a citizen of India, and he must satisfy one of the following: (i) he has been a judge of a High Court, or of two or more such courts in succession, for at least five years; or (ii) he has been an advocate of a High Court, or of two or more such courts in succession, for at least ten years; or (iii) he is, in the opinion of the President, a distinguished jurist.

Official Answer

Q19. Original Jurisdiction means the power to hear a case in the first instance, not on appeal. It covers: (i) a dispute between the Government of India and one or more States; (ii) a dispute between the Government of India and any State or States on one side and one or more other States on the other; (iii) a dispute between two or more States. Under Article 32 the Supreme Court also has original jurisdiction to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Official Answer

Q20. (i) Constitutional cases — an appeal lies where the High Court certifies that the case involves a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution. (ii) Civil cases — where the High Court certifies that the case involves a substantial question of law of general importance which in its opinion needs to be decided by the Supreme Court. (iii) Criminal cases — where the High Court has, on appeal, reversed an order of acquittal and sentenced the accused to death; or has withdrawn a case from a subordinate court, convicted the accused and sentenced him to death; or certifies that the case is a fit one for appeal to the Supreme Court.

Official Answer

Q21. Advisory Jurisdiction — under Article 143 the President may refer to the Supreme Court any question of law or of fact of public importance on which he thinks it expedient to obtain its opinion. The Court may hear the matter and report its opinion to him, but that opinion is purely advisory: it does not bind the President, and the Court is itself not obliged to give one.

Revisory Jurisdiction — under Article 137 the Supreme Court has the power to review any judgement pronounced or order made by it, in order to remove any error that may have crept in. This power exists because the Court's decisions are binding as law on every court in India, so an uncorrected error would be perpetuated throughout the country.

Official Answer

Q22. (i) Its proceedings and judgements are recorded and preserved permanently, and are printed and published. (ii) Those recorded judgements are cited as precedents in all the courts of the country and are binding on them; their authority cannot be questioned in any court. (iii) As a Court of Record it has the power to punish for contempt of itself, so that its dignity and authority are protected. In this way it acts as the guardian of the Constitution.

Official Answer

Q23. Judicial Review is the power of the Supreme Court to examine the constitutional validity of a law passed by Parliament or a State Legislature, or of an order of the executive, and to declare it null and void if it is found to be inconsistent with the Constitution. The Court exercises this power when a law is challenged before it, particularly where the law infringes a Fundamental Right. It is what makes the Supreme Court the guardian of the Constitution and the protector of the Fundamental Rights of citizens, and it keeps both the Legislature and the Executive within the limits set by the Constitution.

Official Answer

Q24. (i) Security of tenure — a judge holds office until a fixed retiring age and can be removed only on proved misbehaviour or incapacity, by an address of both Houses passed by a special majority; the executive alone cannot remove him. (ii) Security of salary — the salaries and allowances of judges are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, are not subject to the vote of Parliament, and cannot be varied to a judge's disadvantage after his appointment. (iii) The conduct of a judge in the discharge of his duties cannot be discussed in Parliament except upon a motion for his removal; the Court can punish for contempt; and a retired judge of the Supreme Court is forbidden to plead or act before any court or authority in India, so that he cannot be tempted by the prospect of practice or office afterwards.

Official Answer

Q25. A judge can be removed only on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity. An address praying for his removal must be presented to the President by each House of Parliament in the same session. Each House must pass it by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting. The charge is first investigated by a committee of enquiry, and the judge is given a chance to defend himself. Only when both Houses have passed the address does the President make the order of removal. The difficulty of this procedure is itself one of the guarantees of judicial independence.

Q26. (i) Habeas Corpus — 'to have the body': it directs the person detaining another to produce him before the Court and justify the detention, and secures his release if the detention is unlawful. (ii) Mandamus — 'we command': it orders a public official, corporation or lower court to perform a public duty it has refused or failed to perform. (iii) Prohibition — issued by a higher court to a lower court or tribunal, forbidding it to continue with proceedings that lie outside its jurisdiction. (iv) Certiorari — 'to be certified': the higher court quashes an order already passed by a lower court or tribunal that acted beyond its jurisdiction or against the principles of natural justice, or transfers the case to itself. (The fifth is Quo Warranto — 'by what authority' — restraining a person from holding a public office to which he is not entitled.)
