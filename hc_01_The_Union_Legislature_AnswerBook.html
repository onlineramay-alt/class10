Q37. The quorum is the minimum number of members who must be present before the House can transact business. In the Indian Parliament it is one-tenth of the total membership of the House, including the Presiding Officer — that is, 55 in the Lok Sabha and 25 in the Rajya Sabha. If there is no quorum, it is the duty of the Speaker either to adjourn the House or to suspend the sitting until there is a quorum.

D. Structured / Eight-Mark Questions

Q45. (i) A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can neither reject nor amend it — it may only recommend changes and must return the Bill within fourteen days. A motion of No-Confidence can be moved and passed only in the Lok Sabha, and the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to it alone. In a deadlock between the two Houses, the matter is decided at a joint sitting, where the will of the Lok Sabha prevails because of its far greater numerical strength.

(ii) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, who is the Vice-President of India, adjourns the House; in his absence, the Deputy Chairman. The House has a maximum of 250 members — up to 238 representatives of the States and Union Territories, elected indirectly by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies by proportional representation through the single transferable vote, and 12 members nominated by the President from persons distinguished in literature, science, art and social service.

(iii) Any four of: it makes laws on all the subjects of the Union List; it makes laws on the subjects of the Concurrent List, and in a conflict the Union law prevails; it exercises the residuary powers, making laws on matters not enumerated in any of the three Lists; it may legislate on a State List subject when the Rajya Sabha so resolves by a two-thirds majority, during a National Emergency, or when two or more States request it; and every ordinance promulgated by the President must be approved by Parliament.

Official Answer

Q46. (i) Money Bills originate only in the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend them; the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha alone and can be removed by a No-Confidence Motion passed there; and at a joint sitting the view of the Lok Sabha prevails because it has roughly twice the membership of the Rajya Sabha.

(ii) Under Article 249 it may by a two-thirds resolution empower Parliament to legislate on a subject in the State List in the national interest. Under Article 312 it may by a two-thirds resolution authorise the creation of a new All India Service. A resolution for the removal of the Vice-President can be initiated only in the Rajya Sabha.

(iii) Any four of: he presides over the sittings of the House and maintains order and decorum; he interprets and applies the rules of procedure, and his decision is final; he decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill, and his certificate on the point is conclusive; he exercises a casting vote in the event of a tie; he presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses; and he decides questions of disqualification of members on the ground of defection.

Q47. (i) Question Hour — members may question ministers on any aspect of administration, and ministers are bound to reply. (ii) Adjournment Motion — the House may set aside its normal business to censure the government over a matter of urgent public importance. (ii) During the operation of a Proclamation of National Emergency (Article 250). (iii) When the Legislatures of two or more States request Parliament by resolution to legislate on a State subject for them (Article 252). Q49. (i) No tax can be levied or collected except by authority of a law passed by Parliament. (ii) The annual budget — the Annual Financial Statement — must be laid before Parliament, and the demands for grants require its approval; it may reduce or refuse a demand by moving a cut motion. (iii) No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India without an appropriation made by Parliament. (Also acceptable: it examines the reports of the Comptroller and Auditor-General through the Public Accounts Committee.)

Official Answer

Q50. Judicial powers: (i) it can impeach the President for violation of the Constitution; (ii) it can pass a resolution for the removal of a Judge of the Supreme Court or of a High Court, and it may punish any person, member or outsider, for contempt or breach of its privileges.

Electoral powers: (i) the elected members of both Houses form part of the Electoral College which elects the President; (ii) the members of both Houses together elect the Vice-President. (The Lok Sabha also elects its own Speaker and Deputy Speaker, and the Rajya Sabha its Deputy Chairman.)

Official Answer

Q51. (i) A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha. (ii) A No-Confidence Motion against the Council of Ministers can be moved and passed only in the Lok Sabha. (iii) The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha alone, and remains in office only so long as it enjoys the confidence of that House. (iv) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting of both Houses. (Also acceptable: the Lok Sabha alone can pass a resolution for the discontinuance of a Proclamation of Emergency.)

Official Answer

Q52. (a) Legislative powers: an ordinary Bill may be introduced in either House and must be passed by both, so in ordinary legislation the two Houses are almost equal. If they disagree, the President may summon a joint sitting, where the Lok Sabha's view prevails because of its greater numbers. The Rajya Sabha alone, however, may authorise Parliament to legislate on a State subject or to create a new All India Service.

(b) Financial powers: here the Lok Sabha is clearly supreme. A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the President's recommendation. After being passed there, it goes to the Rajya Sabha, which must return it within fourteen days and can only make recommendations; the Lok Sabha may accept or reject those recommendations, and if the Bill is not returned in time it is deemed passed by both Houses. The budget is therefore effectively controlled by the Lok Sabha alone.

Q53. The representatives of the States are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies by proportional representation through the single transferable vote; the remaining 12 members are nominated by the President.

Two exclusive powers: it alone may resolve, by a two-thirds majority, that Parliament should legislate on a State List subject in the national interest (Article 249); and it alone may resolve to create a new All India Service (Article 312).

Advantages: these powers exist because the Rajya Sabha represents the States, so any encroachment on State subjects requires the consent of the States' own chamber — this protects the federal balance. Being a permanent House it also gives the Legislature continuity when the Lok Sabha is dissolved, and as a revising chamber it allows a second, more considered look at legislation passed in haste.
