B. Terminology and Very Short Answer

Q3. (a) LET — a stoppage in play after which the rally is replayed and the score stands. Given, for example, when the server serves before the receiver is ready, when a shuttle from another court enters, or when the shuttle lodges in the net after passing over it in play.

(b) FAULT — an infringement that ends the rally and gives the point to the opponent. Examples: the shuttle landing outside the court, failing to pass the net, being hit twice or by a player's body or clothing, a player touching the net with racket or body, or an illegal service.

(c) DEUCE — the situation at 20–20, where a side must then win by a margin of TWO clear points. If the score reaches 29–29, the side scoring the 30th point wins the game.

(d) SMASH — the principal attacking stroke: a powerful overhead stroke struck steeply downward into the opponents' court, the fastest shot in the game.

(e) DROP SHOT — a stroke played gently so that the shuttle just clears the net and falls steeply close to it in the opponents' forecourt, used to draw an opponent forward out of position.

(Also worth knowing: rally, clear or lob, drive, net shot, service court, and the rally-point scoring system.)

Q4. GOVERNING BODIES.
National: the BADMINTON ASSOCIATION OF INDIA (BAI).
International: the BADMINTON WORLD FEDERATION (BWF), formerly the International Badminton Federation.

SCORING. The RALLY-POINT system: a point is scored on every rally, whoever served. A game is played to 21 POINTS, and a match is the BEST OF THREE GAMES.

A game must be won by a margin of at least TWO points, so at 20–20 (deuce) play continues until one side leads by two. If the score reaches 29–29, the side that scores the 30th point wins the game. The side winning a game serves first in the next game.

Q7. (i) GRIP. The FOREHAND grip is the 'handshake' — the racket held as though shaking hands, the V between thumb and forefinger along the narrow edge, held loosely and tightened only at impact. The BACKHAND grip turns the thumb up flat against the wider surface for support. A relaxed grip is essential; a tight one destroys the wrist action from which all power comes.

(ii) SERVICE. The HIGH SERVE, struck underarm so the shuttle travels high and drops steeply near the opponent's back line, used chiefly in singles. The LOW or SHORT SERVE, played gently so the shuttle just skims the net and falls behind the short service line, used chiefly in doubles. The shuttle must be struck below the waist with the racket head below the hand.

(iii) STROKES. The CLEAR or lob — hit high and deep to the back of the opponent's court, defensively to gain time or offensively to push him back. The DROP SHOT — gently played to fall just over the net. The SMASH — struck steeply downward with full power, the game's winning stroke. The DRIVE — a fast flat stroke passing close over the net.

(iv) FOOTWORK. Perhaps the most important skill of all. From the base position at the centre of the court, the player moves to the shuttle with a lunge, chassé or scissor jump, plays the stroke, and RETURNS TO THE BASE at once. Good footwork lets a player reach the shuttle early and play it from a balanced position — a player who is late is forced into a defensive stroke whatever their technique.
