B. Short Answer Type

Q3. The scale of a map is the fixed ratio between a distance measured on the map and the corresponding distance on the ground. It is what makes a map a measured representation rather than a picture.

There are three methods of showing it:

(i) STATEMENT OF SCALE — expressed in words, for example '2 cm to 1 km' or '1 inch to 1 mile'. It is simple, but it can only be used by someone who works in those units.

(ii) REPRESENTATIVE FRACTION (R.F.) — expressed as a ratio without units, for example 1:50,000. This is the universally accepted method.

(iii) LINEAR or GRAPHICAL SCALE — a line drawn on the map, divided and marked in ground distances. Its great advantage is that it enlarges or reduces along with the map if the map is photocopied at a different size, so it never becomes wrong.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q4. The Representative Fraction is the scale of a map expressed as a fraction or ratio in which the numerator is one unit of distance on the map and the denominator is the number of the SAME units on the ground. Thus 1:50,000 means that one centimetre on the map represents 50,000 centimetres on the ground, and equally that one inch represents 50,000 inches.

Its advantage is precisely that it carries no units. A statement of scale in inches and miles is useless to a person who works in centimetres and kilometres; an R.F. can be read by anyone anywhere, and converted into whatever units the reader prefers. That is why it is the internationally accepted method and why it is printed on every Survey of India sheet.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q5. METHOD — bring both sides to the SAME unit (centimetres are easiest), then divide both sides by the map figure so that the numerator becomes 1.

(a) 25 cm = 5 km = 5 × 100,000 cm = 500,000 cm.
Divide both by 25: 1 cm = 20,000 cm. **R.F. = 1:20,000**

(b) 7 cm = 63,000 m = 63,000 × 100 cm = 6,300,000 cm.
Divide both by 7: 1 cm = 900,000 cm. **R.F. = 1:900,000**

(c) 5 cm = 500 m = 500 × 100 cm = 50,000 cm.
Divide both by 5: 1 cm = 10,000 cm. **R.F. = 1:10,000**

(d) 15 cm = 6 km = 6 × 100,000 cm = 600,000 cm.
Divide both by 15: 1 cm = 40,000 cm. **R.F. = 1:40,000**

CONVERSIONS TO REMEMBER: 1 km = 1,000 m = 100,000 cm; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 mile = 63,360 inches.

Q6. METHOD — the R.F. says that 1 unit on the map equals N of the same unit on the ground. Convert N into the unit asked for.

(a) 1:63,360. There are 63,360 inches in a mile, so 1 inch on the map = 63,360 inches = 1 mile.
**Statement: 1 inch to 1 mile.**

(b) 1:1,000,000. 1 cm on the map = 1,000,000 cm on the ground. 1,000,000 cm ÷ 100,000 = 10 km.
**Statement: 1 cm to 10 km.**

(c) 1:100. 1 cm on the map = 100 cm on the ground = 1 metre.
**Statement: 1 cm to 1 metre.**

Always show the division. The mark scheme credits the conversion step even where the final figure is mis-copied.

Q7. DIRECTION ON A MAP. By convention the top of the map is north, and a north line or arrow is printed in the margin to confirm it. Directions between two points are then given by the eight cardinal points — north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west, north-west — measured from the first place towards the second. To find the direction of B from A, place yourself at A and see in which of the eight directions B lies.

THE THREE NORTHS. True North is the direction of the geographic North Pole, along a line of longitude. Magnetic North is the direction in which a compass needle points, towards the magnetic pole, which is not in the same place and which slowly moves. Grid North is the direction of the vertical grid lines on the map sheet.

MAGNETIC DECLINATION is the angle between True North and Magnetic North at a given place and time. It must be allowed for whenever a compass bearing is transferred to a map, and because the magnetic pole shifts, the declination is printed on the sheet together with the year and the annual rate of change.

WHY TRUE AND GRID NORTH DIFFER. Lines of longitude converge on the pole, whereas the grid lines on a map sheet are drawn parallel to each other. The two can therefore coincide only along one central meridian; everywhere else the grid lines make a small angle with the true meridian, and that angle is called the grid variation.

Q8. (a) The Survey of India sheet shows the scale in three ways, so any two of: the STATEMENT of scale (2 centimetres to 1 kilometre); the REPRESENTATIVE FRACTION (1:50,000); and the LINEAR or GRAPHICAL scale, the divided line printed in the margin.

(b) The representative fraction, because it is expressed as a pure ratio with no units attached. One unit on the map represents 50,000 of the same units on the ground, whatever those units are — so a reader working in inches and a reader working in centimetres can both use it without conversion, and it is therefore intelligible internationally.

Official Answer

Q9. (a) METHOD. 'Direct' means a straight line, so use a ruler, not the twine. Measure the straight-line distance between the two settlements in centimetres, then convert using the scale: 2 cm = 1 km, so divide the measurement by 2. Show the working — for example, 'measured distance 7.4 cm; 7.4 ÷ 2 = 3.7 km'. Give the answer in kilometres to one decimal place.

Use the twine only where the question asks for the distance ALONG a road or a river — lay the twine along the winding route, mark it, straighten it against the ruler, and then convert.

(b) METHOD. Find Gulabganj on the sheet and place yourself there. Remembering that the top of the sheet is north, see in which of the eight cardinal directions Pithapura lies from it, and name that direction. The direction must be given FROM the first place named TO the second — reversing them is the standard error and loses the mark.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q10. METHOD. On a 1:50,000 sheet each grid square measures 2 cm by 2 cm on the map and therefore 1 km by 1 km on the ground — that is, each grid square is exactly 1 square kilometre.

So count the number of eastings columns and the number of northings rows on the extract and multiply. If the extract runs from easting 30 to easting 40 and northing 96 to northing 04, that is 10 columns by 8 rows, giving 80 square kilometres.

Show the multiplication. The whole question rests on knowing that one grid square equals one square kilometre, and once that is stated the arithmetic is trivial.
