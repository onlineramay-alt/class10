B. Short Answer Type

Q3. ENDOMORPH. Round and soft in build, with a large abdomen, wide hips, short and tapering limbs, a short neck and a high proportion of body fat. The bones are heavy and the body is comparatively soft. Such a person gains weight easily and loses it with difficulty, and is generally slower in movement.
SUITED TO: sumo wrestling, heavyweight weightlifting, shot put, discus, and the throwing events generally — where mass itself is an advantage. Also long-distance swimming, where buoyancy and insulation help.

MESOMORPH. Muscular and hard in build, with broad shoulders, a narrow waist, a large chest, strong limbs and heavy bones, with little body fat. The classic athletic build. Such a person gains muscle readily and is naturally strong and fast.
SUITED TO: sprinting, gymnastics, wrestling, boxing, weightlifting, football, hockey and most team games — almost anything demanding power, speed and agility.

ECTOMORPH. Lean, thin and linear, with narrow shoulders and hips, long thin limbs, a flat chest, little muscle and little fat, and light bones. Such a person finds it hard to gain either muscle or weight.
SUITED TO: long-distance running and marathon, high jump, and basketball — events rewarding light body weight, a high surface-area-to-mass ratio for heat loss, and length of limb.

THE QUALIFICATION WORTH ADDING: these are extremes of a continuum, and pure types are rare. Most people are a blend with one component dominant, and Sheldon's system in fact rates every individual on all three on a seven-point scale. Body type indicates a natural ADVANTAGE, not a limit — training, technique and determination decide far more than build.
