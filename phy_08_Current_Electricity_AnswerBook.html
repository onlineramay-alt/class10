Q66. Ohm's law: At constant temperature, the current (I) through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends: V ∝ I, or V/I = R (constant), hence V = IR. Circuit for verification: Connect the unknown resistance R in series with an ammeter (A), a rheostat (Rh), a key (K), and a battery. Connect a voltmeter (V) in parallel across R. The rheostat varies the current; record corresponding V and I values. Plot V vs I graph — a straight line through the origin confirms Ohm's law. The slope of the V-I graph gives resistance R.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q67. (a) V-I graph: A straight line through the origin with positive slope — V increases linearly with I. (b) The slope (V/I) of the V-I graph represents the resistance R of the conductor. A steeper slope means higher resistance. (c) At temperature T₁ (lower), the line is less steep; at T₂ (higher), the line is steeper. Conclusion: Resistance of a metallic conductor increases with increase in temperature (steeper slope = greater V/I = greater R).

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q68. An ohmic resistor is one that obeys Ohm's law — the ratio V/I remains constant at constant temperature. Example: nichrome wire, copper wire. I-V graph: A straight line passing through the origin — I increases linearly with V. Determination of resistance: R = 1/slope of the I-V graph = ΔV/ΔI. Alternatively, from a V-I graph, resistance = slope = ΔV/ΔI.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q69. Non-ohmic resistors do not obey Ohm's law — the ratio V/I is not constant; it changes with the applied voltage or current. Examples: junction diode, LED, filament of a bulb, solar cell. I-V graph for a junction diode: The graph is not a straight line — it shows an exponential increase in the forward-biased region and negligible current in reverse bias. For a bulb filament: the graph is a curve (resistance increases as temperature rises with current). In all cases, the I-V (or V-I) graph is non-linear.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q70. E.m.f. (ε): The work done by the cell per unit positive charge in driving it around the entire circuit (including inside the cell). It is the maximum potential difference when no current is drawn. Terminal voltage (V): The potential difference measured across the terminals of the cell when current is flowing. V = ε − Ir. Internal resistance (r): The resistance offered by the electrolyte and electrodes to the flow of current within the cell. Open vs closed circuit: In open circuit (no current), no voltage drop across r, so terminal voltage = ε. In closed circuit (current I flows), there is a voltage drop Ir inside the cell, so terminal voltage V = ε − Ir < ε. Hence terminal voltage is less in a closed circuit.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q71. In series, resistors are connected end to end — the same current I flows through each. The total potential difference V across the combination equals the sum: V = V₁ + V₂. Applying Ohm's law: V₁ = IR₁ and V₂ = IR₂. So V = IR₁ + IR₂ = I(R₁ + R₂). If R is the equivalent resistance: V = IR. Therefore R = R₁ + R₂. For n resistors in series: R = R₁ + R₂ + … + Rₙ. The equivalent resistance is always greater than any individual resistance.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q72. In parallel, both terminals of each resistor are connected to the same two nodes — the same voltage V appears across each. Total current I = I₁ + I₂. By Ohm's law: I₁ = V/R₁ and I₂ = V/R₂. So I = V/R₁ + V/R₂ = V(1/R₁ + 1/R₂). If R is the equivalent resistance: I = V/R. Therefore 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂. For n resistors: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … + 1/Rₙ. The equivalent resistance is always less than the smallest individual resistance.

Q73. Heat produced H = I²Rt (Joule's law). Factors: (i) Current (I): H ∝ I² — doubling the current produces four times the heat. (ii) Resistance (R): H ∝ R — greater resistance produces more heat for the same current. (iii) Time (t): H ∝ t — heat is proportional to the duration of current flow. Joule's law: The heat produced in a conductor is directly proportional to (i) the square of current, (ii) the resistance of the conductor, and (iii) the time for which current flows. H = I²Rt joules = 0.24 I²Rt calories.

Q76. (a) In series: same current I flows through all bulbs. Power P = I²R. A lower-rated bulb (e.g., 40 W, 220 V) has higher resistance (R = V²/P; lower P → higher R). Its V-I graph is a straight line passing through the origin, and a metallic wire such as nichrome at constant temperature is an example. Its V-I graph is a curve and does not stay straight; a junction diode is an example, since it conducts freely in one direction and hardly at all in the other.

Q142. In a series arrangement the same current I flows through every resistor, and the total potential difference across the combination equals the sum of the potential differences across the separate resistors.
