B. Terminology and Very Short Answer

Q3. (a) OFFSIDE — a player is in an offside POSITION if he is nearer the opponents' goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent, in the opponents' half, at the moment the ball is played to him by a team-mate. It is an offence only if he is then involved in active play. A player is not offside from a throw-in, a corner kick or a goal kick.

(b) DRIBBLING — running with the ball under close control, using short touches of the inside, outside or instep of the foot to keep it within playing distance while beating opponents.

(c) TACKLING — dispossessing an opponent of the ball using the feet, fairly and without endangering him. A tackle from behind or one that endangers safety is a foul.

(d) HAT-TRICK — three goals scored by the same player in a single match.

(e) CORNER KICK — awarded to the attacking side when the ball crosses the goal line, not between the posts, having last been touched by a defender. It is taken from the quarter-circle at the nearest corner flag.

(Also worth knowing: throw-in, goal kick, free kick — direct and indirect, penalty kick, header, volley, advantage rule.)

Q6. (i) KICKING or PASSING. The INSIDE-OF-THE-FOOT push pass for accuracy over short distances — the non-kicking foot placed beside the ball, ankle locked, follow-through towards the target. The INSTEP drive for power and distance, struck with the laces. The OUTSIDE of the foot for a disguised or curling pass, and the lofted pass struck under the ball.

(ii) DRIBBLING. Keeping the ball within playing distance with short touches while running, using the inside and outside of both feet, the head up to see the field, and a change of pace or direction — a feint, a step-over, a drag-back — to beat the defender.

(iii) TRAPPING or RECEIVING. Bringing the ball under control with the foot, thigh or chest by CUSHIONING it — withdrawing the receiving surface at the moment of contact so the ball drops dead rather than bouncing away — and turning it at once into the direction of the next move.

(iv) HEADING. Meeting the ball with the FOREHEAD, not the top of the head, with the eyes open and the neck firm, the whole trunk driving through the ball from the waist. Used to clear defensively (high and wide), to score (downwards, into the ground), and to pass.

(Also acceptable: TACKLING — the block tackle and the sliding tackle; THROW-IN — both feet on the ground, ball delivered from behind and over the head with both hands; and GOALKEEPING — positioning, catching, punching, diving and distribution.)
