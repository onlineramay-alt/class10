D. Descriptive Type Questions

Q45. (a) Phytohormones are naturally occurring, small organic molecules produced by plants in one part that, even in very low concentrations, regulate growth and other physiological processes in another part.
(b) Tropism is an oriented growth movement of a plant organ in response to (towards or away from) a directional/unidirectional external stimulus.
(c) A Clinostat is a rotating apparatus used to demonstrate geotropism -- by continuously rotating a potted seedling, gravity acts equally on all sides so the plant's normal directional geotropic response is neutralised.
(d) Apical dominance is the phenomenon in which the terminal (apical) bud of a plant, through the auxin it produces, suppresses the growth of lateral (axillary) buds below it.
(e) Parthenocarpy is the development of fruit from the ovary without fertilization taking place, resulting in seedless fruit.
(f) Abscission is the natural shedding/falling off of plant parts such as leaves, flowers or fruits, usually as a result of the formation of an abscission layer at their base, promoted by hormones such as abscisic acid and ethylene.
(g) Heliotropism (solar tracking) is the directional growth/turning movement of plant parts, such as the flower head of sunflower, that follow the daily path of the sun across the sky.

Official Answer

Q46. (a) Thigmotropism is a growth response to the stimulus of touch/contact with a solid object; Geotropism is a growth response to the stimulus of gravity.
(b) Positive tropism is growth movement TOWARDS the direction of the stimulus; Negative tropism is growth movement AWAY from the direction of the stimulus.
(c) A Stimulus is any change in the external (or internal) environment that is capable of evoking a response in an organism; a Response is the reaction shown by the organism as a result of that stimulus.
(d) Phototropism is a growth response to the stimulus of light; Chemotropism is a growth response to the stimulus of chemicals (e.g. growth of the pollen tube towards chemicals secreted by the ovule).

Official Answer
