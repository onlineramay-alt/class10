Q8. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

I have a mind myself and recognize
Mind when I meet with it in any guise.
No one can know how glad I am to find
On any sheet the least display of mind.

(i) What is the poet doing when he notices the speck, and what does he at first take it to be? [3]
(ii) Describe the behaviour of the mite that convinces the poet it is intelligent. [3]
(iii) Why does the poet decide to spare it? [3]
(iv) 'On any sheet the least display of mind' — explain the double meaning of 'sheet' here. [3]
(v) What is the poet satirising in this poem? [4]

Q9. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

When something strange about it made me think,
This was no dust speck by my breathing blown,
But unmistakably a living mite
With inclinations it could call its own.

(i) What was strange about the speck that attracted the speaker's attention? What was it in reality? Describe the speaker's attitude towards it. [3]
(ii) What made the speaker realise that the mite had a mind of its own and intelligence? Give three reasons. [3]
(iii) Analyse the speaker's decision to let the mite live. What does it reveal about his character? [3]
(iv) How would you evaluate the significance of the poem's ending? Why is the discovery of a 'display of mind' so important to the speaker? [3]
(v) What is the central theme of the poem, and how does the speaker's action illustrate it? If you had to give the poem a different title, what would it be, and why? [4]
