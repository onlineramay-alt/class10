Act V is the second most examined part of the play after Act III: the papers on record set extract questions from Act 5 Scene 1, Scene 3 and Scene 5. Note that the act divides into a parley (Scene i), Cassius's death (Scene iii) and Brutus's death with Antony's eulogy (Scene v) — and that the two deaths are deliberately contrasted.

Q5. Read the extract and answer the questions that follow:

ANTONY:
This was the noblest Roman of them all:
All the conspirators save only he
Did that they did in envy of great Caesar;
He only, in a general honest thought
And common good to all, made one of them.
His life was gentle, and the elements
So mix'd in him that Nature might stand up
And say to all the world 'This was a man!'
(Act V, Scene v)

(i) Of whom is Antony speaking, and in what circumstances? [3]
(ii) On what grounds does Antony distinguish this man from all the other conspirators? [3]
(iii) Explain 'the elements / So mix'd in him'. [3]
(iv) What orders does Octavius give at the close of the play? [3]
(v) Why is it significant that this tribute is spoken by Antony? [4]
