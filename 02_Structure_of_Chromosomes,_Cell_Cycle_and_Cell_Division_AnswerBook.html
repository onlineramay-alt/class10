D. Descriptive Type Questions

Q78. (a) Chromosome: a long, highly coiled and condensed chromatin fibre made of DNA (about 40%) and histone proteins (about 60%), present in the nucleus, that carries the chemical instructions for reproduction of the cell. (b) Gene: a specific sequence of nucleotides on a chromosome that encodes a particular protein/feature; the unit of heredity transferred from parents to offspring. (c) Cell division: the process by which a cell divides and its duplicated chromosomes are evenly distributed into the daughter cells. (d) Chromatid: one of the two identical strands of a duplicated chromosome, joined to its sister chromatid at the centromere, formed when the DNA molecule is copied during replication. (e) Aster: the star-shaped arrangement of radiating microtubule rays around each centriole, formed after the centrosome splits into two during mitosis in an animal cell.

Official Answer

Q79. (a) Meiosis halves the chromosome number, so when male and female gametes fuse during fertilisation, the normal diploid number of chromosomes is restored in the offspring rather than doubling with each generation. (b) Meiosis is called 'reductional division' because the chromosome number is reduced to half — out of 23 pairs of chromosomes in humans, only a single set (haploid, 'n') is passed on to the sex cells, as opposed to the diploid ('2n') number in body cells. (c) The recombination/mixing up of genes during meiotic division (through crossing over and independent assortment) produces innumerable variations in the offspring, so children of the same parents differ from each other even though they share the same parents.

Official Answer

Q80. (a) Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm and occurs after karyokinesis, forming two daughter cells; karyokinesis divides the nucleus first, forming two nuclei. (b) DNA is double-stranded, contains Thymine, and is found mainly in the nucleus/mitochondria; RNA is single-stranded, contains Uracil instead of Thymine, and is found in the cytoplasm/nucleus/ribosomes. (c) A nucleosome is DNA wrapped around a core of histone proteins; a nucleotide is the single repeating unit of phosphate + sugar + nitrogenous base that makes up the DNA strand. (d) A centrosome is an organelle near the nucleus containing centrioles that move to the poles and form spindle fibres; a centromere is the non-stainable region of the chromosome where the two chromatids join and spindle fibres attach. (e) A haploid (n) cell has only one member of each chromosome pair (half the full number), found in gametes after meiosis; a diploid (2n) cell has the full set of chromosome pairs, found in body cells after mitosis. (f) A karyotype is the complete, ordered set of an individual's chromosomes displayed/photographed in pairs by size; karyokinesis is the process of nuclear division during cell division. (g) Autosomes are the 22 pairs of chromosomes not involved in sex determination; sex chromosomes (allosomes) are the 1 pair (X and Y) that determine the sex of the individual. (h) A chromosome pair is homozygous at a locus when it carries identical alleles of a gene at the corresponding position on both homologous chromosomes; it is heterozygous when the two alleles at that locus differ.

Q81. About 99% of human DNA lying between genes does not code for any protein (sometimes called 'junk' or non-coding DNA). However, this non-coding DNA varies enormously from person to person, and this individual-specific variation is the basis of DNA fingerprinting — a technique used to identify individuals, for example in forensic investigations and paternity testing.
