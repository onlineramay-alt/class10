The three facts this chapter rests on: GST is charged on the price AFTER discount; CGST and SGST are each half the GST rate for a sale within a state; and Input Tax Credit means a dealer pays the government only the difference between the tax he collects and the tax he paid.

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (c) 18%.

Rate of GST = (GST paid ÷ marked price) × 100
= (2160 ÷ 12000) × 100
= 0.18 × 100
= **18%**

Check: 18% of 12,000 = 2,160. ✓

Official Answer

Q2. (b) 6% and 6%.

For a sale WITHIN a state (intra-state), the GST is shared equally between the Centre and the State, so CGST = SGST = half the GST rate = 12 ÷ 2 = 6% each.

For a sale BETWEEN states (inter-state), IGST is charged at the full rate — 12% — and there is no CGST or SGST.

B. Worked Problems

Q3. STEP 1 — the purchase (Mumbai to Delhi: INTER-state, so IGST).
Cost price for the shopkeeper = ₹20,000.
IGST paid = 18% of 20,000 = (18/100) × 20,000 = **₹3,600**

STEP 2 — the selling price.
Profit = 15% of 20,000 = ₹3,000
Selling price = 20,000 + 3,000 = ₹23,000

STEP 3 — the sale (Delhi to Delhi: INTRA-state, so CGST and SGST at 9% each).
CGST = 9% of 23,000 = (9/100) × 23,000 = **₹2,070**
SGST = 9% of 23,000 = **₹2,070**
Total GST collected = 2,070 + 2,070 = ₹4,140

STEP 4 — net GST payable, using INPUT TAX CREDIT.
The shopkeeper has already paid ₹3,600 as IGST on his purchase, and may set that off against the tax he has collected.
Net GST payable = GST collected − GST paid
= 4,140 − 3,600
= **₹540**

NOTE the two things examiners check: that INTER-state attracts IGST while INTRA-state splits into CGST + SGST at half the rate each; and that the dealer pays the government only the DIFFERENCE, never the whole tax he collected.

Q4. STEP 1 — the retailer's purchase (UP to UP: INTRA-state).
Discount = 10% of 40,000 = ₹4,000
Price paid by the retailer = 40,000 − 4,000 = ₹36,000

GST is charged on the price AFTER discount — this is the point of the question.
GST paid by the retailer = 12% of 36,000 = (12/100) × 36,000 = ₹4,320
(As it is intra-state this is CGST ₹2,160 + SGST ₹2,160.)

STEP 2 — the retailer's sale (UP to Bihar: INTER-state, so IGST).
Selling price = printed price = ₹40,000
IGST collected = 12% of 40,000 = ₹4,800

STEP 3 — net GST payable by the retailer.
= GST collected − GST paid (input tax credit)
= 4,800 − 4,320
= **₹480**

SHORTCUT WORTH KNOWING: the net GST a dealer pays is always the GST rate applied to HIS PROFIT. Here the profit is 40,000 − 36,000 = ₹4,000, and 12% of 4,000 = ₹480. ✓ Use it to check the long answer.

Q5. The ₹5,900 is the price INCLUDING tax, so it represents 118% of the pre-tax price — not 100%. This is the standard trap.

Let the price before GST be ₹x.
Then x + 18% of x = 5,900
x (1 + 0.18) = 5,900
1.18x = 5,900
x = 5,900 ÷ 1.18 = **₹5,000**

(ii) GST = 5,900 − 5,000 = **₹900**

Check: 18% of 5,000 = 900, and 5,000 + 900 = 5,900. ✓

WATCH: taking 18% of 5,900 gives ₹1,062, which is wrong. When a price is stated as 'including GST', divide by (1 + rate), never multiply.
