1. DRAW THE DIAGRAM. Mark the right angle, the given lengths and the given angles. Most lost marks in this chapter are lost before any trigonometry is done.
2. Identify which sides you have and which you want, and choose the ratio accordingly — SOH CAH TOA.

ANGLE OF ELEVATION is measured UPWARD from the horizontal; ANGLE OF DEPRESSION is measured DOWNWARD from the horizontal. The angle of depression from the top of a tower to a point equals the angle of elevation from that point to the top — alternate angles between parallel horizontals.

B. Worked Problems

Q3. Let the height of the tower be h metres.

In the right-angled triangle, the tower is the side OPPOSITE the angle of 30°, and the 60 m is the side ADJACENT to it. Opposite and adjacent means TANGENT.

tan 30° = opposite/adjacent = h/60

1/√3 = h/60

h = 60/√3

Rationalise:
h = 60/√3 × √3/√3 = 60√3/3 = 20√3

20 × 1.732 = 34.64

**Height = 34.64 m** (to two decimal places)

SANITY CHECK: the angle is less than 45°, so the height must be LESS than the base distance. 34.64 < 60 ✓ If the angle had been 60°, the height would have exceeded 60 m.

Q4. Let the height be h, the foot of the tower B, the nearer point C (angle 60°) and the farther point D (angle 30°), with CD = 40 m.

FROM THE NEARER POINT C:
tan 60° = h/BC
√3 = h/BC
BC = h/√3 …(i)

FROM THE FARTHER POINT D:
tan 30° = h/BD
1/√3 = h/BD
BD = h√3 …(ii)

But BD = BC + CD = BC + 40. Substituting (i) and (ii):
h√3 = h/√3 + 40

Multiply throughout by √3 to clear the fraction:
3h = h + 40√3
2h = 40√3
h = 20√3

20 × 1.732 = 34.64

**Height = 34.64 m** (to two decimal places)

CHECK: BC = h/√3 = 20√3/√3 = 20 m, and BD = h√3 = 20 × 3 = 60 m. The difference BD − BC = 60 − 20 = 40 m ✓ — which is the given distance between the points.

THE PATTERN: two angles from two points give two equations in h and one unknown distance; eliminate the distance and solve for h. Almost every question of this type works exactly so.

Q5. The ANGLE OF DEPRESSION from the top of the cliff to the boat is 45°. By alternate angles between the horizontal at the top and the horizontal water surface, the ANGLE OF ELEVATION from the boat to the top of the cliff is also 45°.

Let the distance of the boat from the foot be d metres.

tan 45° = opposite/adjacent = 100/d

1 = 100/d

**d = 100 m**

NOTE the special case: when the angle is 45°, tan 45° = 1, so the horizontal distance EQUALS the vertical height. That is worth remembering as an instant check — a 45° sighting always gives an isosceles right-angled triangle.

WATCH THE COMMON ERROR: using the angle of depression as though it were inside the triangle at the top vertex without first transferring it. Always mark the horizontal dashed line at the top and use alternate angles.

Q6. Let the building be h m and the flagpole f m.
From the point to the foot of the flagpole: tan 31° = h/100 → h = 100 × 0.6009 = 60.09 m.
From the point to the top of the flagpole: tan 33° = (h + f)/100 → h + f = 100 × 0.6494 = 64.94 m.

Flagpole f = 64.94 − 60.09 = 4.85 ≈ **5 m**.

Q11. Let the height be h, and let the nearer point be at distance x from the foot.

tan 45° = h/(x + 20) → x + 20 = h.

So h/√3 + 20 = h → 20 = h(1 − 1/√3) = h(1 − 0.5774) = 0.4226 h.
