D. Descriptive Type

Q77. (a) Excretion is the elimination of substances that are no longer useful to the body, or those that are harmful if allowed to accumulate.
(b) Kidney is a paired, bean-shaped excretory organ located on either side of the backbone, which filters nitrogenous waste from the blood, regulates blood pressure/composition, and maintains the body's water and mineral balance.
(c) Micturition is the process by which urine is expelled from the urinary bladder through the urethra, brought about by relaxation of the sphincter muscles under nervous system control.
(d) Osmoregulation is the regulation of the osmotic pressure of the blood/body fluids by the kidney, through control of the amount of water and dissolved salts (electrolytes) it retains or excretes.

Q79. (a) Excretion is necessary because metabolic waste products (like carbon dioxide and urea) are toxic if allowed to accumulate in the body, and their timely removal prevents poisoning and cellular damage, keeping the internal chemical environment stable.
(b) The cortex of the kidney shows a dotted appearance because it contains numerous Bowman's capsules (Malpighian corpuscles) and the coiled proximal/distal convoluted tubules of the nephrons, scattered throughout it.
(c) In summer, more water is lost through sweating, so the kidney tubules reabsorb a greater proportion of water from the glomerular filtrate to conserve it — this results in less, and more concentrated (thicker), urine being passed; the opposite happens in winter.
(d) The medulla shows a striped appearance because it contains the Loops of Henle and collecting ducts running in straight, parallel lines, bundled together into renal pyramids.

Official Answer

Q81. Ultrafiltration is the filtration of blood plasma under high pressure through the walls of the glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule, allowing water, glucose, salts and nitrogenous wastes to pass through while retaining blood cells and large plasma proteins.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q82. In cholera, severe vomiting and diarrhoea cause extreme dehydration. To conserve the body's dwindling water supply, the kidneys reabsorb an unusually large amount of water (and, along with it, reabsorb more urea than normal) from the glomerular filtrate. This drastically reduces urine output and allows toxic urea to accumulate in the blood — a condition called uremia, which can be fatal.

Official Answer

Q83. Gout is a painful condition caused by the crystallisation of excess uric acid (which has low solubility in water) in the joints, especially of the toes and fingers, leading to inflammation and pain.

Official Answer

Q84. Kidney stones are hard deposits formed inside the kidney when excessive uric acid and salts (such as calcium oxalate) crystallise out of concentrated urine instead of remaining dissolved.

Official Answer

Q85. Dialysis works on the principle of diffusion of small molecules across a semi-permeable membrane. The patient's blood is passed through fine tubes made of a selectively permeable membrane, immersed in a dialysing fluid whose osmotic pressure equals that of normal blood. Small molecules such as urea, excess salts and other wastes diffuse out of the blood, across the membrane, into the dialysing fluid, while blood cells and large proteins are retained — purifying the blood before it is returned to the patient's vein.
