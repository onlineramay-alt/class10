SECTION FORMULA — the point dividing the join of A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio m : n is

( (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n) , (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n) )

Note which subscript goes with which letter: m multiplies the SECOND point. Getting this the wrong way round divides in the ratio n : m instead, and is the commonest error in the chapter.

MIDPOINT (ratio 1 : 1): ( (x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2 )
CENTROID of a triangle: ( (x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3 , (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3 )

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (c) C, the mid-point of the hypotenuse AB.

The point equidistant from all three vertices of any triangle is its CIRCUMCENTRE — the centre of the circle passing through all three.

In a RIGHT-ANGLED triangle the circumcentre lies at the MID-POINT OF THE HYPOTENUSE. This follows from the converse of the angle-in-a-semicircle theorem: since ∠AOB = 90°, the point O lies on the circle with AB as diameter, so the centre of that circle is the mid-point of AB, and it is equidistant from A, O and B.

The coordinates would then be found by the mid-point formula applied to A and B.

B. Worked Problems

Q2. Here (x₁, y₁) = (−3, 4), (x₂, y₂) = (5, −2), m = 3, n = 1.

x = (mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n)
= [3(5) + 1(−3)] / (3 + 1)
= (15 − 3)/4
= 12/4
= **3**

y = (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)
= [3(−2) + 1(4)] / 4
= (−6 + 4)/4
= −2/4
= **−1/2**

The point is **(3, −1/2)**.

SANITY CHECK: the ratio 3 : 1 means the point is three-quarters of the way from A to B, so it should be much nearer B than A. A has x = −3 and B has x = 5; the answer x = 3 is indeed close to 5 ✓ If your answer had come out near −3, you would have used the ratio the wrong way round.

Q3. MID-POINT M of A(2, −3) and B(6, 7):
x = (2 + 6)/2 = 8/2 = 4
y = (−3 + 7)/2 = 4/2 = 2
M = **(4, 2)**

CENTROID of the triangle with vertices (1, 2), (3, −4), (5, 8):
x = (1 + 3 + 5)/3 = 9/3 = 3
y = (2 − 4 + 8)/3 = 6/3 = 2
Centroid = **(3, 2)**

The centroid formula is simply the average of the three x-coordinates and the average of the three y-coordinates — worth remembering in that form, because it is impossible to get the subscripts wrong.

Q4. Let P divide AB in the ratio k : 1. Then by the section formula, using the x-coordinates:

x = [k(8) + 1(−4)] / (k + 1) = 2

8k − 4 = 2(k + 1)
8k − 4 = 2k + 2
6k = 6
**k = 1**

So the ratio is 1 : 1.

VERIFY with the y-coordinates, which must give the same ratio:
y = [1(8) + 1(2)] / (1 + 1) = 10/2 = 5 ✓

**P divides AB in the ratio 1 : 1** — that is, P is the mid-point of AB.

ALWAYS CHECK WITH THE OTHER COORDINATE. If the x and y coordinates give different ratios, the point does not lie on the line at all, and that is itself sometimes what a question is testing.
