Official Answer

Q28. (i) The President is only the nominal head of the State; the real powers are exercised by the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers. A directly elected President would claim a popular mandate of his own and could set himself up as a rival to the Prime Minister, upsetting the parliamentary system. (ii) A direct election over an electorate of hundreds of millions would be enormously expensive and would take a great deal of time, for an office that is largely ceremonial. (iii) The indirect method still gives the President a representative character, because those who elect him — Members of Parliament and of the State Legislative Assemblies — are themselves elected by the people; and it helps keep the office above the level of party politics.

Official Answer

Q29. (i) He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha: he presides over its sittings, maintains order, decides points of order, and exercises a casting vote in the event of a tie. (ii) He acts as President when a vacancy occurs in that office by reason of death, resignation or removal, and continues until a new President is elected — which must be within six months. (iii) He discharges the functions of the President when the President is temporarily unable to do so, for instance through illness or absence abroad.

Official Answer

Q30. (i) He presides over the sittings of the Rajya Sabha and conducts its business, maintaining order and decorum. (ii) He interprets and applies the rules of procedure of the House, and decides points of order; his ruling is final. (iii) He does not vote in the first instance but exercises a casting vote when the votes on a question are equally divided. (He also decides, in the Rajya Sabha, questions of disqualification of members on the ground of defection.)

Official Answer

Q31. The President can be removed only for violation of the Constitution. The charge may be preferred by either House of Parliament. The resolution must be signed by not less than one-fourth of the total members of that House, and fourteen days' notice must be given to the President. It must then be passed by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of that House. The charge is then investigated by the other House, and the President has the right to appear and to be represented at that investigation. If the second House also passes the resolution by not less than two-thirds of its total membership, the President stands removed from the date on which that resolution is passed.

Official Answer

Q32. A resolution for his removal must be moved in the Rajya Sabha, after at least fourteen days' notice, and must be passed by a majority of all the then members of that House. It must then be agreed to by the Lok Sabha. Unlike the President, no ground such as 'violation of the Constitution' has to be proved, and the process is not called impeachment.

Q33. Q34. Each of these is exercised on the advice of the Prime Minister and the Cabinet, which is exactly why they show him to be a nominal head: (i) he addresses the first session of Parliament after each general election and the first session of each year, and may send messages to either House — but the address is written by the government; (ii) he summons and prorogues the Houses of Parliament and may dissolve the Lok Sabha; (iii) he gives his assent to a Bill so that it becomes law, and he nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha. (Also acceptable: his prior recommendation is needed for the introduction of a Money Bill, and he may promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session.)

Official Answer

Q39. (i) He appoints the Chief Justice and the other judges of the Supreme Court and of the High Courts. (ii) Under Article 72 he has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites and remissions of punishment, and to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of an offence — including a sentence of death and a sentence passed by a court-martial. (iii) Under Article 143 he may refer any question of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court for its opinion, though that opinion is only advisory and does not bind him. D. Structured / Eight-Mark Questions

Q40. (i) He must be a citizen of India; he must have completed 35 years of age; and he must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha. He must also not hold any office of profit.

(ii) Appointing the Prime Minister when no party has a clear majority in the Lok Sabha; refusing the advice of a Council of Ministers that has lost its majority, for instance on a request to dissolve the House; and the suspensive veto — returning a Bill other than a Money Bill for reconsideration, or withholding assent from it altogether.

(iii) The President may be removed only for violation of the Constitution. The charge may be preferred by either House. The resolution must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total members of that House, fourteen days' notice must be given to the President, and it must be passed by not less than two-thirds of the total membership of that House. The other House then investigates the charge, and the President has the right to appear and be represented. If that House also passes the resolution by two-thirds of its total membership, the President is removed from the date of that resolution.

Official Answer

Q41. (i) He is elected indirectly, by an Electoral College, in accordance with the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, the voting being by secret ballot. The Electoral College consists of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States, including those of Delhi and Puducherry; nominated members do not take part.

(ii) See the discretionary powers above — appointment of a Prime Minister in a hung Parliament; refusal to act on the advice of a Council of Ministers that has lost the confidence of the House; and the suspensive and pocket vetoes over Bills.

(iii) All executive action of the Union is taken in his name; he appoints the Prime Minister and, on his advice, the other ministers; he appoints the Governors, the Attorney-General, the Comptroller and Auditor-General, the Chief Election Commissioner and the judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts; and he is the Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces of India.

Official Answer

Q42. (a) The President is the nominal head of the State, the real power resting with the Prime Minister and the Cabinet; a directly elected President would claim his own popular mandate and could rival the Prime Minister. A direct election of an electorate of hundreds of millions would also be very costly and slow. The indirect method still leaves him representative, since his electors are themselves elected, and it keeps the office above party politics.

(b) The Constitution empowers him to proclaim three kinds of emergency. A National Emergency under Article 352, on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion, during which the Union may legislate on State subjects and the Fundamental Rights under Article 19 may be suspended. A Constitutional Emergency under Article 356, or President's Rule, where the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution, so that the State's administration is taken over by the Union. A Financial Emergency under Article 360, where the financial stability or credit of India is threatened, during which the salaries of public servants may be reduced and money bills of the States reserved for the President's consideration.

Official Answer

Q43. (i) The charge of violating the Constitution may be preferred by either House; the resolution needs the signatures of one-fourth of that House's total members and fourteen days' notice, and must be passed by two-thirds of its total membership. The other House then investigates the charge, the President having the right to appear and be represented, and if it too passes the resolution by two-thirds of its total membership he is removed.

(ii) He presides over the sittings of the House and maintains order; he interprets and applies its rules of procedure and decides points of order, his ruling being final; and he exercises a casting vote when the votes are equally divided.

(iii) Financial — a Money Bill can be introduced only on his recommendation, and the annual budget is laid before Parliament on his behalf. Executive — he appoints the Prime Minister and, on his advice, the other ministers, and he is the Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces of India.

Official Answer

Q44. (i) National or General Emergency under Article 352, on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion; Constitutional Emergency or President's Rule under Article 356, on the failure of the constitutional machinery in a State; and Financial Emergency under Article 360, when the financial stability or credit of India is threatened.

(ii) He summons and prorogues the Houses and may dissolve the Lok Sabha; he addresses the first session of Parliament after a general election and the first session of each year, and may send messages to either House; and no Bill becomes an Act without his assent, he being entitled to return a Bill other than a Money Bill for reconsideration. He also nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha and may promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session.

(iii) Two reasons: the office is a nominal one, so a direct popular mandate would create a rival to the Prime Minister and distort the parliamentary system; and a direct election on so vast a scale would be extremely expensive and time-consuming. The Electoral College consists of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament together with the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States and of Delhi and Puducherry.
