B. Terminology and Very Short Answer

Q3. (a) LBW — Leg Before Wicket. A batsman is out if the ball, without first touching the bat, strikes any part of his body when it would otherwise have gone on to hit the stumps, provided the ball pitched in line or outside off stump and struck him in line with the wicket (with an exception if no shot was offered).

(b) MAIDEN OVER — an over of six legal deliveries from which no run is scored off the bat and no extras are conceded.

(c) HAT-TRICK — a bowler taking three wickets with three consecutive deliveries, which may span two overs or even two innings.

(d) GOOGLY — a delivery by a leg-spinner that is bowled with an apparent leg-break action but turns the OTHER way, from off to leg for a right-handed batsman. Also called a 'wrong 'un'.

(e) YORKER — a fast delivery pitched right up to the batsman, aimed to land at the base of the stumps or on his toes, giving him almost no time to bring the bat down.

(Other terms worth knowing: no ball, wide, duck, silly point, gully, night watchman, follow-on, declaration.)

Q5. WHAT THE DIAGRAM MUST SHOW, with these measurements labelled:

THE FIELD. Oval or circular, with no fixed dimensions in the Laws — the boundary is normally between 65 and 90 yards (about 59 to 82 m) from the centre of the pitch. Mark the boundary line and the 30-yard (27.4 m) inner circle used in limited-overs cricket.

THE PITCH, at the centre. A rectangle 22 YARDS (20.12 m) long between the two sets of stumps, and 10 FEET (3.05 m) wide.

AT EACH END:
— the BOWLING CREASE, 8 feet 8 inches (2.64 m) long, with the stumps in its centre;
— the POPPING CREASE, 4 FEET (1.22 m) in front of the bowling crease and parallel to it, unlimited in length but marked to at least 6 feet either side;
— the two RETURN CREASES, at right angles to the bowling crease, 4 feet 4 inches (1.32 m) either side of the middle stump, running back at least 8 feet.

THE STUMPS. Three per end, together 9 INCHES (22.86 cm) wide, each 28 INCHES (71.1 cm) above the ground, with two bails on top, each 4⅜ inches long.

MARK ALSO the two ends by name — the bowler's end and the striker's end.

THE MARKS ARE FOR THE MEASUREMENTS AND THE LABELS, not the artistry. A neat rectangle with every figure written on it scores full marks; a beautiful oval with no numbers scores almost nothing.

Q6. (i) BATTING. The grip — both hands close together on the handle, the V between thumb and forefinger of each hand in line with the splice. The stance — side-on, feet either side of the popping crease, knees slightly flexed, eyes level and watching the bowler's hand. The strokes — front-foot drives (off, cover, straight, on), the forward defensive, and the back-foot strokes (cut, pull, hook, back defensive). The head must be still and over the line of the ball.

(ii) BOWLING. A rhythmic run-up, a side-on or front-on delivery stride, a high arm action with the front arm pulling down, release with the wrist behind the ball, and a full follow-through. FAST bowling relies on pace, swing and seam; SPIN bowling on revolutions imparted by fingers (off-spin) or wrist (leg-spin), varying flight, length and turn.

(iii) FIELDING. Attacking the ball, getting the body behind it, and the long barrier for a hard-hit ball. Catching — high catches with fingers up, low catches with fingers down, always watching the ball into the hands. Throwing — an accurate flat throw over the stumps, and the underarm flick when close in.

(iv) WICKET-KEEPING. Crouching low with the weight forward and gloves together on the ground, rising with the ball, taking it with soft hands and giving with it. Standing UP to the stumps for spinners, allowing stumpings; standing BACK to fast bowlers. Also directing the field and backing up throws.
