THE THEOREMS THIS CHAPTER RESTS ON:

ANGLE PROPERTIES — the angle at the CENTRE is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc; angles in the SAME SEGMENT are equal; the angle in a SEMICIRCLE is 90°.

CYCLIC PROPERTIES — opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are SUPPLEMENTARY (sum to 180°); the EXTERIOR angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the INTERIOR OPPOSITE angle.

TANGENT PROPERTIES — a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact; the two tangents from an external point are equal; the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the ALTERNATE SEGMENT.

Every circle question is one or two of these applied in sequence. Name the theorem you are using at each step — marks are given for the reason as well as the figure.

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (d) 130°.

STEP 1 — use the EXTERIOR ANGLE property of a cyclic quadrilateral: the exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.
∠CDE is the exterior angle at D, and the interior opposite angle is ∠ABC.
So ∠ABC = 65°.

STEP 2 — use the ANGLE AT THE CENTRE property: the angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle it subtends at the circumference.
∠ABC is at the circumference on arc AC, and ∠AOC is at the centre on the same arc.
∠AOC = 2 × ∠ABC = 2 × 65° = **130°**

Option (b) forgets to double; option (a) halves instead of doubling; option (c) is the supplement, which would be the answer if the question had asked for the opposite interior angle.

B. Worked Problems

Q2. ANGLE ACB — C is on the MAJOR arc, so ∠ACB stands on the same arc AB as the central angle.
By the angle-at-the-centre theorem, the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference:
∠ACB = ½ × ∠AOB = ½ × 80° = **40°**

ANGLE ADB — D is on the MINOR arc, so ACBD is a cyclic quadrilateral and ∠ADB is opposite ∠ACB.
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary:
∠ADB = 180° − 40° = **140°**

NOTE the distinction the question is testing. A chord subtends EQUAL angles anywhere on the SAME arc, but SUPPLEMENTARY angles on the two DIFFERENT arcs. Always check which side of the chord the point lies on before applying the theorem.

Q3. STEP 1 — find ∠OAP and ∠OBP.
A tangent is PERPENDICULAR to the radius at the point of contact.
**∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°**

STEP 2 — find ∠AOB using the angle sum of the quadrilateral OAPB.
The angles of any quadrilateral sum to 360°:
∠AOB + ∠OAP + ∠APB + ∠OBP = 360°
∠AOB + 90° + 70° + 90° = 360°
∠AOB + 250° = 360°
**∠AOB = 110°**

CHECK by a shortcut worth remembering: in this configuration ∠AOB and ∠APB are always SUPPLEMENTARY, because the two right angles account for 180° of the quadrilateral. 110° + 70° = 180° ✓

Q4. STEP 1 — ∠ACB.
AB is a DIAMETER, so ∠ACB is the angle in a SEMICIRCLE.
**∠ACB = 90°** (angle in a semicircle is a right angle)

STEP 2 — ∠ABC.
In triangle ABC the angles sum to 180°:
∠BAC + ∠ACB + ∠ABC = 180°
35° + 90° + ∠ABC = 180°
∠ABC = 180° − 125°
**∠ABC = 55°**

GIVE THE REASONS, not just the numbers — 'angle in a semicircle' and 'angle sum of a triangle'. In geometry questions the reason usually carries as much credit as the value, and an unreasoned answer is marked down even when correct.
