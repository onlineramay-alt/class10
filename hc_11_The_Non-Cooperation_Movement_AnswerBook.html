C. Short Answer Type

Q12. (i) The Rowlatt Act, 1919. It empowered the government to arrest and detain without warrant and to try political cases without a jury and without appeal, and it was pushed through in the face of the unanimous opposition of the Indian members. A government that legislated so against the whole of Indian opinion had shown what the post-war reforms were worth.

(ii) The Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the Punjab wrongs. On 13 April 1919 General Dyer fired without warning on an unarmed crowd penned in an enclosed garden, killing 379 by the official count and wounding over a thousand; martial law followed, with public floggings and the notorious crawling order. That Dyer was then defended in the House of Lords and presented with a public purse in England destroyed what remained of Indian faith in British justice.

(iii) The Khilafat grievance. Indian Muslims were bitterly angered by the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire and the humiliation of the Sultan, whom they revered as Khalifa. Gandhi made their cause his own, which brought Hindus and Muslims into a single movement as nothing had before. To these may be added the disappointment of the Montagu-Chelmsford reforms of 1919, whose dyarchy fell far short of what had been expected in return for India's wartime sacrifices.

Official Answer

Q13. The negative side was the withdrawal of Indian cooperation from the machinery of British rule: the surrender of titles and honorary offices; the boycott of government schools and colleges, of the law courts, of the reformed legislative councils and of the elections to them; the refusal to serve in the army or to attend official functions; the boycott of foreign goods, especially foreign cloth; and, at a later stage, the refusal to pay taxes.

The positive side was the constructive programme, without which the first would have been merely destructive: national schools and colleges were founded — Jamia Millia Islamia, the Kashi Vidyapith, the Gujarat Vidyapith; panchayats were set up to settle disputes out of court; hand-spinning and khadi were promoted to give the villages work and to make the boycott of foreign cloth possible; and Gandhi insisted throughout on Hindu-Muslim unity, the removal of untouchability, temperance and, above all, strict non-violence.

Q14. Because of the Chauri Chaura incident. On 5 February 1922, in the Gorakhpur district, the police fired on a procession of volunteers; the crowd turned on them, drove them into the police station and set it alight, and 22 policemen were burnt to death.

For Gandhi non-violence was not a tactic to be judged by results but the very principle of the movement, and a struggle that had produced such an act was, in his words, going in the wrong direction. He also believed the people were not yet sufficiently disciplined in non-violence for a mass campaign, and that if the movement continued it would degenerate into general violence, which the government would then crush with far greater bloodshed. He therefore had the Congress Working Committee suspend the movement at Bardoli on 12 February 1922, over the protests of Nehru, Bose and most of his colleagues. He was arrested in March, tried for sedition and sentenced to six years' imprisonment.

Official Answer

Q15. (i) It turned the national movement into a mass movement. Until 1920 the Congress had been an annual gathering of the educated few; now peasants, workers, students, shopkeepers and women joined in their millions, in every province, and the Congress was reorganised at Nagpur into a body with village and district committees, a membership fee of four annas and work conducted in the regional languages. Nationalism reached the villages for the first time.

(ii) It achieved a degree of Hindu-Muslim unity never seen before or since, with the Khilafat and Congress causes fused into one and Muslim divines sharing platforms with Congress leaders.

(iii) It shook the prestige of British rule and revealed its dependence on Indian consent. Titles were surrendered, courts and colleges emptied, imports of foreign cloth fell by half, and the government was forced to see that it governed only because Indians allowed it to. Gandhi emerged as the undisputed leader of the nation, and his method of non-violent non-cooperation became the accepted instrument of the struggle.

Against this must be set the fact that Swaraj was not obtained within a year as promised, that the sudden withdrawal disheartened many workers, and that Hindu-Muslim unity did not survive the collapse of the Khilafat cause when Turkey itself abolished the Caliphate in 1924.

Q18. (i) It was the agitation of Indian Muslims, led by the Ali brothers, against the Allied treatment of the Sultan of Turkey, whom they revered as Khalifa, after the Treaty of Sèvres stripped him of territory and authority. Gandhi supported it because he saw in it the opportunity of a lifetime to unite Hindus and Muslims in a single national struggle — a grievance felt passionately by one community, taken up as a matter of justice by the whole nation, would forge a unity that no constitutional arrangement could.

(ii) Negatively: surrender of titles and honorary offices; boycott of government schools, colleges, courts, legislative councils and elections; refusal to serve in the army or attend official functions; boycott of foreign goods; and later, refusal to pay taxes. Positively: national schools and colleges such as Jamia Millia Islamia and the Gujarat Vidyapith; panchayats to settle disputes; hand-spinning and khadi; Hindu-Muslim unity, removal of untouchability and temperance — the whole to be carried out with strict non-violence.

(iii) The Chauri Chaura incident of 5 February 1922. Gandhi's reasoning was that non-violence was the movement's principle and not its tactic; that the burning of the police station showed the people were not yet ready for a disciplined mass struggle; and that continuing would invite both general violence and a repression far bloodier than anything the movement could withstand. He suspended it at Bardoli on 12 February, against the wishes of nearly all his colleagues.
