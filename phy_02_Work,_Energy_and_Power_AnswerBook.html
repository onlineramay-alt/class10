Q99. (a) Positive work: When the force and displacement are in the same direction (or component of force is in the direction of displacement). Example: A person pushing a box on a floor — force acts forward, box moves forward, work done is positive. (b) Negative work: When the force and displacement are in opposite directions. Example: A fielder stopping a cricket ball — the fielder's force is directed backward (opposite to ball's motion), so work done by fielder on the ball is negative. Also, when a body is lifted, gravity does negative work (gravity acts downward, displacement is upward).

Official Answer

Q100. When a body of mass m falls through a height h:
- Force of gravity = mg (downward)
- Displacement = h (downward, same direction as force)
- θ = 0° between force and displacement
Work done by gravity W = F × S × cosθ = mg × h × cos0° = mgh.
Thus, the work done by gravity when a body falls through height h is W = mgh (positive).

Official Answer

Q101. Power P = Work done / Time = Force × velocity. Two factors: (i) Amount of work done (or energy transferred): Greater the work done in the same time, greater the power. Example: A motor that lifts 1000 kg higher than another motor of the same weight in the same time develops more power. (ii) Time taken to do the work: Lesser the time taken to do the same work, greater the power. Example: A boy who climbs up stairs in 10 s develops more power than one who does the same in 20 s.

Official Answer

Q102. Gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by a body by virtue of its position above a reference level (e.g., the ground). Derivation: To raise a body of mass m to height h, we apply a force equal to its weight (mg) upward through displacement h (upward). Work done against gravity = Force × displacement = mg × h. This work is stored as gravitational potential energy. Therefore, Gravitational PE = mgh.

Q106. Let body of mass m fall from height h. Take ground as reference (PE = 0).
(i) At the top: KE = 0 (starts from rest), PE = mgh. Total ME = mgh.
(ii) After falling distance x (height above ground = h−x): velocity v₁ where v₁² = 2gx. KE = ½mv₁² = ½m(2gx) = mgx. PE = mg(h−x). Total ME = mgx + mg(h−x) = mgh.
(iii) At ground: height = 0, PE = 0. v² = 2gh. KE = ½mv² = ½m(2gh) = mgh. PE = 0. Total ME = mgh.
In all three cases, Total ME = mgh = constant. Hence total mechanical energy is conserved.

Q195. Let a body of mass m be released from rest at a height h. At the start its potential energy is mgh and its kinetic energy is zero, so the total is mgh. After falling a distance x its height is (h − x), so U = mg(h − x). Using v² = 2gx, its kinetic energy is K = ½mv² = ½m(2gx) = mgx. The total is therefore mg(h − x) + mgx = mgh. Just before striking the ground x = h, so U = 0 and K = mgh. At every point the total is mgh, which verifies that U + K is constant.
