Q8. (a) Because crude oil is bulky and expensive to transport, and refining reduces it to lighter, more valuable products. A refinery placed AT THE OILFIELD — Digboi, for instance — saves the cost of carrying crude any distance. A refinery placed AT A PORT — Mumbai, Kochi, Chennai, Visakhapatnam — is sited where imported crude is landed, since India imports most of its oil and it is cheaper to bring crude by tanker to the coast and refine it there than to move it inland. Refineries in either position also have the pipelines, storage and transport links that the trade requires.

(b) Because fossil fuels are exhaustible and are running out, while sunlight is inexhaustible and free; because burning coal and oil releases carbon dioxide and other pollutants and is a chief cause of climate change, whereas solar power is clean; because the cost of solar panels has fallen sharply, making solar power cheaper than new coal generation in India; and because solar can be installed where it is needed — on a roof, in a village far from the grid — which suits a country with a great deal of sunshine and many remote settlements.

Official Answer

Q9. ADVANTAGES. India has very large reserves, chiefly in the Damodar valley and the Son–Mahanadi belt, sufficient for a long time. It is comparatively cheap and the technology to use it is well established. It is the basis of thermal power, which generates most of India's electricity, and coking coal is indispensable to the smelting of iron. It also yields valuable by-products — coal gas, coal tar, ammonia, benzol — used in the chemical, plastics, dye and fertilizer industries. It can be stored and transported without difficulty.

DISADVANTAGES. It is exhaustible and non-renewable. Burning it releases carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide and particulates, so it is a major cause of air pollution, acid rain and global warming. Mining scars the landscape, causes subsidence, and is dangerous work. Indian coal is of comparatively poor quality, with high ash content and low calorific value, and there is a shortage of good coking coal, which must be imported. It is bulky, so transport costs are high, and most reserves are concentrated in the east, far from the industrial west and south.

Q10. LOCATION. The Bhakra dam is built across the river SUTLEJ at Bhakra in Himachal Pradesh, with the Nangal dam a short distance downstream in Punjab. It is one of the highest gravity dams in the world, and its reservoir is the Govind Sagar. It is a joint project of Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh and Rajasthan.

THREE BENEFITS. IRRIGATION — its canals irrigate a very large area of Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan, and it was fundamental to the success of the Green Revolution in the north-west. HYDROELECTRIC POWER — its power houses supply electricity to Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan and Delhi, powering both industry and the tube wells on which the region's farming depends. FLOOD CONTROL — it regulates the flow of the Sutlej and has ended the flooding that formerly damaged the plains below.

(Also acceptable: drinking water supply, inland fisheries in the Govind Sagar, and the development of tourism.)
