Q70. Colour vision is the function of the cone cells of the retina, but cones need fairly bright light to function and are inactive in dim light. Hence colours cannot be distinguished in dim light.

D. Descriptive Type Questions

Q74. (a) Conjunctiva: the thin, transparent mucous membrane that covers the front of the sclera and lines the inside of the eyelids. (b) Lysozyme: an antiseptic enzyme present in tears that kills bacteria entering the eye. (c) Adaptation: the ability of the eye to adjust its sensitivity to changing light intensity (dark or light adaptation). (d) Power of accommodation: the ability of the eye lens to change its curvature/focal length so as to focus on objects at different distances. (e) Ear ossicles: the three small bones (malleus, incus, stapes) of the middle ear that transmit and amplify sound vibrations from the eardrum to the oval window.

Official Answer

Q75. (a) Myopia is corrected with a concave (diverging) lens; Hyperopia is corrected with a convex (converging) lens. (b) Rods are highly sensitive and function in dim light (night/monochromatic vision); cones are less sensitive and function only in bright light (colour vision). (c) Aqueous humour is located between the cornea and the lens (in the anterior chamber); vitreous humour fills the large chamber between the lens and the retina. (d) In near accommodation the lens becomes more curved/rounded (thicker); in distant accommodation the lens becomes flatter/thinner. (e) In dark adaptation, rhodopsin (visual purple) is regenerated in the rods; in light adaptation, iodopsin is regenerated in the cones. (f) Night blindness occurs when the rods cannot function properly (due to lack of rhodopsin/vitamin A); colour blindness occurs when the cones cannot function properly.

Official Answer

Q76. (b) Three separate ossicles, linked by a lever-like arrangement, can progressively magnify the small vibrations of the eardrum far more effectively than a single bone could, making faint sounds audible. (c) The blind spot is the point where the optic nerve leaves the retina; it has no rods or cones (sensory cells) at all, so no image can be formed there — hence it is called the 'area of no vision'.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q85. Sound waves are collected by the pinna and travel down the auditory canal to strike and vibrate the tympanum (eardrum). These vibrations are transmitted and amplified by the three ear ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) and passed on through the oval window (fenestra ovalis) to the fluid of the cochlea. The resulting fluid vibrations stimulate the sensory hair cells of the Organ of Corti, which generate nerve impulses that travel via the auditory nerve to the brain.

For viewing near objects, the ciliary muscles contract, slackening the suspensory ligaments; the lens's own elasticity then lets it bulge and become thicker/more rounded, decreasing its focal length.
