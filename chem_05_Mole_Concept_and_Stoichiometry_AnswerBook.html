The only heavily numerical chapter of the course, and the calculations follow a small number of fixed routes. Every one of them goes through the MOLE: convert what you are given into moles, use the equation's coefficients to get moles of what you want, then convert back. The constants needed throughout: 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles = the relative molecular mass in grams = 22.4 litres of any gas at STP.

A. Multiple Choice Questions

Q1. (b) 15 litres.

By Gay-Lussac's law of gaseous volumes, gases react in volumes that bear a simple whole-number ratio, the same as the ratio of the coefficients in the equation. Here 1 volume of C₃H₈ gives 3 volumes of CO₂.

So 5 litres of propane gives 5 × 3 = 15 litres of carbon dioxide.

Note that water is not counted — it is a liquid at STP, not a gas — and that oxygen does not enter the calculation at all, since the question asks only about the carbon dioxide produced.

Molecular mass of O₂ = 2 × 16 = 32 g, and 32 g is one mole, which occupies 22.4 litres at STP.

Moles of O₂ = 8 / 32 = 0.25 mol.
Volume = 0.25 × 22.4 = **5.6 litres.**

Q15. The GRAM MOLECULAR MASS, also called the MOLAR MASS or one MOLE of the substance.

Official Answer

Q16. (a) GAY-LUSSAC'S LAW: when gases react, they do so in volumes which bear a simple whole-number ratio to one another and to the volumes of the gaseous products, provided all volumes are measured under the same conditions of temperature and pressure.

(b) AVOGADRO'S LAW: equal volumes of all gases, under the same conditions of temperature and pressure, contain the same number of molecules.

Q17. STP means STANDARD TEMPERATURE AND PRESSURE — 0 °C (273 K) and 760 mm of mercury (1 atmosphere).

One mole of ANY gas occupies 22.4 litres (22,400 cm³) at STP. This is called the gram molecular volume, and it follows from Avogadro's law: since equal volumes contain equal numbers of molecules, equal numbers of molecules must occupy equal volumes.

(b) The EMPIRICAL FORMULA.

(c) The (relative) MOLECULAR MASS. Since vapour density is the mass of a certain volume of the gas compared with the same volume of hydrogen, and a hydrogen molecule has a mass of 2, the molecular mass is 2 × vapour density.

Q19. (a) MOLAR VOLUME is the volume occupied by one mole (the gram-molecular mass) of any gas, measured at a stated temperature and pressure. At STP it is 22.4 litres (22 400 cm³).

(b) VAPOUR DENSITY is the ratio of the mass of a certain volume of a gas to the mass of the same volume of hydrogen, both measured under the same conditions of temperature and pressure. It has no units, and molecular mass = 2 × vapour density.

C. Numerical Problems

Q20. By AVOGADRO'S LAW, equal volumes of gases under the same conditions contain equal numbers of molecules. So the number of molecules of chlorine equals the number of molecules of ammonia, and we need only count the ammonia.

Molecular mass of NH₃ = 14 + (3 × 1) = 17 g/mol.
Moles of NH₃ = 34 / 17 = 2 mol.
Number of molecules = 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.2044 × 10²⁴ molecules.**

By Avogadro's law the chlorine container holds the same number: **1.2044 × 10²⁴ molecules.**

Note that the atomic mass of chlorine is given only as a distractor — it is not needed, because Avogadro's law makes the identity of the gas irrelevant. Recognising that is the whole point of the question.

Official Answer

Q21. Molecular mass of CO₂ = 12 + (2 × 16) = 44 g/mol.

MOLES = mass / molar mass = 11 / 44 = **0.25 mol.**

MOLECULES = 0.25 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.5055 × 10²³ molecules.**

VOLUME AT STP = 0.25 × 22.4 = **5.6 litres.**

THE THREE ROUTES, which cover most numericals in this chapter:
mass → moles: divide by molar mass.
moles → molecules: multiply by 6.022 × 10²³.
moles → volume at STP: multiply by 22.4 litres.
Each is reversed by doing the opposite.

Q22. STEP 1 — divide each percentage by the atomic mass:
C: 40 / 12 = 3.33

STEP 2 — divide each by the smallest (3.33):

EMPIRICAL FORMULA = **CH₂O.**

STEP 3 — empirical formula mass = 12 + 2 + 16 = 30.
n = molecular mass / empirical formula mass = 180 / 30 = 6.

MOLECULAR FORMULA = (CH₂O)₆ = **C₆H₁₂O₆** (glucose).

THE METHOD IN FOUR STEPS, which never varies: divide by atomic masses; divide by the smallest; multiply up if any ratio is not a whole number; then find n and multiply the empirical formula by it.

Q23. Molar masses: CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100 g/mol; CaO = 40 + 16 = 56 g/mol.

STEP 1 — moles of CaCO₃ = 25 / 100 = 0.25 mol.

STEP 2 — from the equation, 1 mol CaCO₃ gives 1 mol CaO and 1 mol CO₂. So 0.25 mol CaCO₃ gives 0.25 mol CaO and 0.25 mol CO₂.

STEP 3 —
Mass of CaO = 0.25 × 56 = **14 g.**
Volume of CO₂ at STP = 0.25 × 22.4 = **5.6 litres.**

CHECK BY MASS: 25 g of CaCO₃ gives 14 g of CaO plus 11 g of CO₂ (0.25 × 44), and 14 + 11 = 25. Mass is conserved, so the answer is consistent.

Always set the working out in these three steps and show them. Marks are given for the method even when the arithmetic slips.

Q24. By Gay-Lussac's law the volumes are in the same ratio as the coefficients, so divide all volumes by the smallest (45):

Hydrocarbon : oxygen : carbon dioxide = 45 : 225 : 135 = 1 : 5 : 3

Let the hydrocarbon be CₓH_y. The combustion equation is:
CₓH_y + (x + y/4) O₂ → x CO₂ + (y/2) H₂O

Comparing with the ratio 1 : 5 : 3:

From the CO₂ term: x = **3.**
From the O₂ term: x + y/4 = 5, so 3 + y/4 = 5, giving y/4 = 2 and y = **8.**

The hydrocarbon is **C₃H₈ — propane.**

CHECK against the equation in the 2026 paper: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O, which is exactly the ratio 1 : 5 : 3. Water is not counted because it condenses and is not a gas under the stated conditions.

Q38. In 20 litres of the mixture there are 12 litres of propane (60%) and 8 litres of butane (40%).

By Gay-Lussac's law the volume ratios follow the equation coefficients:
Propane: 1 volume gives 3 volumes of CO₂, so 12 litres → 36 litres CO₂.
Butane: 2 volumes give 8 volumes of CO₂, so 8 litres → (8/2) × 8 = 32 litres CO₂.

Total volume of CO₂ = 36 + 32 = **68 litres.**
